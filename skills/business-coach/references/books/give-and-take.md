## THE ONE LINE
Givers finish last AND first — the key is giving strategically without being a doormat.

## THE 3 RECIPROCITY STYLES

```
GIVERS: Contribute to others without expecting anything back
→ Give their time, knowledge, connections
→ Focus on others' interests

TAKERS: Get more than they give
→ Use others as means to an end
→ Charm up, exploit down

MATCHERS: Balance give and take
→ Quid pro quo relationships
→ The most common style

THE COUNTERINTUITIVE FINDING:
Givers are BOTH the most successful AND least successful people.
The difference: Strategic vs. Indiscriminate giving.

FAILED GIVERS: Give indiscriminately, get burned out and used
SUCCESSFUL GIVERS: Give strategically to people who will reciprocate
                   AND give back to their communities
```

## THE 5 MINUTE FAVOR

Rifkin's concept: Give value in 5 minutes or less.

Most giving feels burdensome because people think it requires significant time.
The 5-minute favor reframes: Small gives accumulate into massive goodwill.

EXAMPLES:
→ Forward a relevant article
→ Make a quick introduction
→ Share someone's content
→ Answer a specific question
→ Write a short recommendation
→ Give feedback on something they're working on

DAILY PRACTICE: One 5-minute favor for someone in your network.
Impact over a year: 250+ acts of generosity. Enormous goodwill.

## THE DOORMAT PROBLEM

How givers avoid being exploited:

SCREENING: Not everyone deserves your giving.
Identify takers early: They use "I" instead of "we."
They take credit, blame others. Don't give to confirmed takers.

CHUNK GIVING: Give in big, visible chunks rather than constant small trickles.
Paradoxically: Bigger, less frequent giving feels more significant.

ADVOCATING FOR OTHERS: Much easier than advocating for yourself.
Use your generosity to advocate for people who deserve it.
This creates goodwill without the personal discomfort of self-promotion.

GIVE DIFFERENTLY TO DIFFERENT PEOPLE:
→ Givers: Give generously. They'll amplify your giving.
→ Matchers: Trade fairly. They'll reciprocate.
→ Takers: Be cautious. Give with structure and expectations.
