## THE ONE LINE
Strategy is not a plan — it's an integrated set of choices about where to play and how to win.

## THE STRATEGY CHOICE CASCADE — DEEP

**CHOICE 1: WINNING ASPIRATION**
What does winning look like for YOU specifically?
Not "being the best" — too vague.
Not "making money" — too generic.

Examples:
"Be the #1 operational support system for ecommerce brands under $10M"
"Generate $10M in family cash flow annually by 2033"
"Build the most trusted AI coaching brand for ambitious mothers"

THE TEST: Your aspiration should make some things obviously YES
and make many other things obviously NO.
If your aspiration allows everything: it's not an aspiration.

**CHOICE 2: WHERE TO PLAY**
Which customers? Which channels? Which geographies?
Which segments? Which products/services?

WHERE NOT TO PLAY is as important as where to play.
By choosing a customer: you're unchoosing all others.
By choosing a channel: you're unchoosing all others.

The power of where to play:
A mediocre strategy executed where competition is weak
beats an excellent strategy executed into a competitive stronghold.

**CHOICE 3: HOW TO WIN**
What is your competitive advantage?
Only 2 sustainable advantages exist:
a) Cost advantage: You can deliver cheaper (Walmart, Southwest)
b) Differentiation advantage: You deliver something uniquely valuable (Apple, Hermès)

CRITICAL: You cannot be both. Trying to be both = stuck in the middle.
Stuck in the middle = beaten by cost leaders AND differentiators.

**CHOICE 4: CAPABILITIES**
What must you be excellent at to win where you've chosen to play?
These are specific, concrete capabilities — not generic virtues.

NOT: "great customer service" (generic)
YES: "90-second response time to any client inquiry via Slack, 24/7"

The capabilities must be unusual.
If every competitor has them: they're not advantages.

**CHOICE 5: MANAGEMENT SYSTEMS**
What systems reinforce your strategy?
Metrics, incentives, structures, processes.

IF your strategy says "be the fastest":
But your incentive structure rewards profit margin:
Your strategy is wrong. Or your system is wrong.
They must align.

## THE STRATEGY COHERENCE TEST

Draw your 5 choices.
Connect them with arrows.
Each choice should REINFORCE each other.

If they don't: you don't have a strategy.
You have a list of aspirations.

EXAMPLE OF COHERENT STRATEGY:
Aspiration: #1 janitorial service in Long Beach for commercial properties
Where to play: Long Beach commercial, 5,000+ sqft, repeat contracts
How to win: Differentiation — reliability + technology + communication
Capabilities: Scheduling software, trained staff, real-time reporting to clients
Systems: Jobber platform, weekly client check-ins, performance bonuses tied to client retention

Each choice reinforces the others. That's coherent strategy.
