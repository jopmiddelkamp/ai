## THE PERSONAL IDENTITY AUDIT

Before strategic moves: Know yourself completely.

```
THE 5 QUESTIONS:
1. What do I truly want? (Not what I should want)
2. What am I willing to sacrifice?
3. What is my unique advantage?
4. Where do I perform at my best?
5. What are my non-negotiables?

THE PERSONALITY TYPES:
TYPE A: Lives for the thrill of the chase
→ Loves competition, needs challenges, driven by winning
→ Risk: Moves too fast, undervalues teams

TYPE B: Lives for building
→ Loves creating, needs creative freedom
→ Risk: Poor at selling, may underestimate competition

TYPE C: Lives for contribution  
→ Loves impact, needs purpose
→ Risk: May undervalue financial returns

TYPE D: Lives for leadership
→ Loves influence, needs people around them
→ Risk: May prioritize popularity over results

YOUR TYPE determines your optimal role.
TYPE A: CEO or Top Salesperson
TYPE B: Product or creative lead
TYPE C: Mission-driven organization leader
TYPE D: Team leader or COO
```

## THE WAR ROOM CONCEPT

Bet-David recommends building a personal board of advisors — your "war room."

```
SEAT 1: THE MENTOR (someone 10-20 steps ahead of you)
→ Can see your future from experience
→ Prevents you from making mistakes they already made

SEAT 2: THE PEER (someone at your level, different strengths)
→ Context without hierarchy
→ Peer accountability is different from mentor accountability

SEAT 3: THE DEVIL'S ADVOCATE
→ Argues against every idea
→ Forces you to stress-test your thinking

SEAT 4: THE SPECIALIST (domain expert you're not)
→ Finance, legal, operations, tech — depends on your gaps
→ Compensates for your blind spots

SEAT 5: THE CONNECTOR
→ Knows everyone
→ Opens doors you can't open alone

BUILD YOUR WAR ROOM BEFORE YOU NEED IT.
Finding advisors in a crisis = desperation = bad judgment.
```
