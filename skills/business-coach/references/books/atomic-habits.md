## THE ONE LINE
Tiny changes → remarkable results. Identity shapes behavior more than motivation.

## THE AGGREGATION OF MARGINAL GAINS
British Cycling: 1% improvement in everything.
Bike weight, tire pressure, seat ergonomics, sleep position.
Result: 5 Tour de France victories in 6 years.

YOUR 1% IMPROVEMENTS:
If you improve 1% per day for a year: 37x better.
If you decline 1% per day for a year: 0.03 of what you were.

The math of tiny habits is counterintuitive.
Small improvements feel insignificant. The compound interest isn't visible until later.

## THE HABIT LOOP DEEP DIVE

**CUE TYPES:**
Time: 7am every morning
Location: At my desk
Emotional state: When I feel anxious
Other people: When I see my workout partner
Preceding action: After I pour my coffee

Most powerful cues: Time + Location together.
"At 6am in my home office" is more powerful than "in the morning."

**CRAVING DEEP DIVE:**
You don't crave the habit itself.
You crave the CHANGE IN STATE the habit produces.
You don't crave cigarettes — you crave stress relief.
You don't crave social media — you crave stimulation and distraction.

Design habits that give you the state change you're actually seeking.

**RESPONSE DEEP DIVE:**
The FRICTION of a behavior determines whether it happens.
Make good habits low friction. Make bad habits high friction.

Good habit friction reduction:
→ Put workout clothes next to bed
→ Keep healthy food at eye level in fridge
→ Open the document you need to write the night before

Bad habit friction addition:
→ Delete social apps from home screen
→ Log out of accounts (extra steps = fewer visits)
→ Keep junk food in the car, not the house

**REWARD DEEP DIVE:**
The reward must come IMMEDIATELY for the habit to stick.
Future rewards (health in 20 years) don't work.
Immediate rewards do.

Habit tracker as immediate reward:
Checking the box releases dopamine.
The streak itself becomes the reward.
"Don't break the chain."

## THE IDENTITY SHIFT

Most people: "I want to lose weight" → Set a goal → Try to achieve it.
Atomic Habits: "I am someone who takes care of their body" → Make choices consistent with that identity.

IDENTITY-BASED HABIT BUILDING:
1. Decide who you want to be
2. Prove it to yourself with small wins
3. The evidence accumulates
4. The identity becomes real

"Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become."

THE BUSINESS APPLICATION:
"We are a company that [core value]."
Every decision: Is this consistent with that identity?
Culture is habits at the organizational level.
