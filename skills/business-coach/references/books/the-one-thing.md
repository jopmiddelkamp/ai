## THE ONE LINE
> *"What's the ONE Thing I can do, such that by doing it, everything else will be easier or unnecessary?"*

Extraordinary results come from going *small* — finding the single most important thing you can do right now, and giving it everything.

## THE CORE INSIGHT

```
The lie that defeats most people:        The truth that beats it:
─────────────────────────────             ──────────────────────────────
"I have too much to do."                  Not everything matters equally.
"I should multitask."                     Multitasking is a scam — it
                                          makes you slower and dumber.
"I just need more discipline."            You need ONE habit, not 100.
"I need balance."                         You need counterbalance.
"I should think small to play safe."      Think BIG, act SMALL.
```

> *"Extraordinary success is sequential, not simultaneous."* — Keller & Papasan

## THE FOCUSING QUESTION (the heart of the book)

The book's central tool. Memorise it. Use it daily.

> **What's the ONE Thing I can do / such that by doing it / everything else will be easier or unnecessary?**

### Two forms — use both

```
BIG-PICTURE QUESTION       "What's my ONE Thing?"
                           Use for direction. Strategy. Career.
                           Life. The 5-year arc.

SMALL-FOCUS QUESTION       "What's my ONE Thing RIGHT NOW?"
                           Use for tactics. Today. This task.
                           The next domino.
```

### Why the wording matters

Each clause does work — don't paraphrase:

- *"ONE Thing"* — forces a single answer, not a list.
- *"I can do"* — must be doable now, not "should" or "would."
- *"such that by doing it"* — there has to be a leverage effect.
- *"everything else will be easier or unnecessary"* — the leverage test. If you do this and nothing downstream gets simpler, you haven't found the right ONE Thing yet.

## THE DOMINO EFFECT (Chapter 2)

```
A 2-inch domino can topple a domino 50% larger.
Chain that rule — each domino 1.5× the previous:

 #1   2 inches          (start)
 #10  ≈ Peyton Manning height
 #18  ≈ Leaning Tower of Pisa
 #23  ≈ Eiffel Tower
 #31  ≈ Mount Everest + 3,000 feet
 #57  ≈ Distance from Earth to the Moon

Why this matters: you don't have to be able to knock down the
big domino directly. You have to knock down the SMALL one,
in line with the bigger ones.
```

Source: Lorne Whitehead's 1983 *American Journal of Physics* paper. A San Francisco Exploratorium physicist reproduced the experiment in 2001 with eight plywood dominoes — first 2 inches, last almost 3 feet tall. The fall began with a tick and ended with a loud SLAM.

**The principle:** Find the lead domino. Knock it over. Repeat.

## PART 1 — THE 6 LIES THAT DERAIL US

The book frames the path to extraordinary results as removing six widespread lies. Each is a full chapter. Cite them by name when the user is suffering from one:

```
LIE #1 — EVERYTHING MATTERS EQUALLY                          (Chapter 4)
  Reality: A minority of causes produces the majority of results
  (Pareto's 80/20 — and you should take it FURTHER:
  20% → 5% → ONE).
  A to-do list becomes a SUCCESS list only when you prioritise it.

LIE #2 — MULTITASKING                                        (Chapter 5)
  Stanford's Clifford Nass studied 262 students:
  "Multitaskers were just lousy at everything."
  Workers lose ~28% of an average workday to multitasking.
  Distracted driving = 16% of all US traffic fatalities.
  A phone conversation takes a 40% bite out of focus
  (same impairment as being drunk).

LIE #3 — A DISCIPLINED LIFE                                  (Chapter 6)
  You don't need to be disciplined at everything.
  You need ENOUGH discipline to build ONE right habit.
  University College London 2009 study: average time to form
  a new habit = 66 DAYS (not 21 — that's a myth).
  Range: 18–254 days depending on the habit.

LIE #4 — WILLPOWER IS ALWAYS ON WILL-CALL                    (Chapter 7)
  Willpower is a fast-twitch muscle. Finite per day.
  Mischel's marshmallow test: "high-delayer" kids scored
  ~210 points higher on the SAT.
  Israeli parole judges (Levav et al., 1,112 hearings):
  ~65% favourable rulings after breaks; near-zero by end of session.
  RULE: Do your ONE Thing EARLY — when willpower is full.

LIE #5 — A BALANCED LIFE                                     (Chapter 8)
  Balance is a myth. What you actually do is COUNTERBALANCE.
  Magic happens at the extremes, not in the middle.
  Two buckets: work (counterbalance over the LONG cycle —
  big projects need long focused stretches);
  personal (counterbalance over the SHORT cycle —
  don't neglect family/health/friends for long).
  Patterson: "Work is a rubber ball. The other four —
  family, health, friends, integrity — are made of glass."

LIE #6 — BIG IS BAD                                          (Chapter 9)
  Fear big and you'll either avoid it or sabotage it.
  Dweck's growth mindset vs fixed mindset.
  Sabeer Bhatia arrived in the US with $250 and built Hotmail
  (sold to Microsoft for $400M).
  Sam Walton's estate planning while still small saved his family
  an estimated $11–13B in inheritance tax later.
  THINK BIG, ACT BOLD, FEAR MEDIOCRITY.
```

## PART 2 — GOAL SETTING TO THE NOW (Chapter 14)

The book's signature goal-cascading framework. Use this when the user is overwhelmed by where to start.

```
SOMEDAY GOAL
    ↓  What's the ONE Thing I want to do someday?
FIVE-YEAR GOAL
    ↓  Based on my Someday Goal,
       what's the ONE Thing I can do in the next 5 years?
ONE-YEAR GOAL
    ↓  Based on my Five-Year Goal,
       what's the ONE Thing I can do this year?
MONTHLY GOAL
    ↓  Based on my One-Year Goal,
       what's the ONE Thing I can do this month?
WEEKLY GOAL
    ↓  Based on my Monthly Goal,
       what's the ONE Thing I can do this week?
DAILY GOAL
    ↓  Based on my Weekly Goal,
       what's the ONE Thing I can do today?
RIGHT NOW
       Based on my Daily Goal,
       what's the ONE Thing I can do RIGHT NOW?
```

**Why all seven levels matter:** skip steps and the question loses leverage. Try asking *"What's the ONE Thing I can do right now to hit my someday goal?"* — the gap is too wide to answer. You need the cascade to bridge today's action to lifetime ambition.

**The "Planning Fallacy" research backs this up.** Three studies of 262 students: those who visualised the PROCESS (every step they'd need) outperformed those who only visualised the OUTCOME. Goal Setting to the Now is process visualisation, structured.

**Written goals work.** Gail Matthews' Dominican University study (n=267): people who wrote down their goals were 39.5% more likely to accomplish them; those who also sent weekly progress to a friend were 76.7% more likely.

## PART 3 — TIME BLOCKING (Chapter 15)

If Goal Setting to the Now tells you WHAT, time blocking tells you WHEN.

### The three things to time block, in order

```
1. TIME BLOCK YOUR TIME OFF
   Successful people see themselves as working BETWEEN vacations.
   Plan rest first so it actually happens.

2. TIME BLOCK YOUR ONE THING
   Aim for FOUR HOURS A DAY, early.
   Stephen King writes mornings: "Mornings belong to whatever
   is new — the current composition."
   This is the minimum — not the maximum.
   To master a domain, expect ~10,000 hours of deliberate practice
   (Ericsson, 1993). Four hours/day × ~250 workdays/year = ~10 years.

3. TIME BLOCK YOUR PLANNING TIME
   Weekly review of monthly/annual goals.
   Annual review late enough to see the year's arc.
```

### Protect the time block

The world doesn't know your priorities. Protect your block as if it were unmovable. The book recommends a sign for your workspace:

> *"Until my ONE Thing is done — everything else is a distraction."*

### Maker vs Manager (Paul Graham)

```
MAKER TIME      Half-day blocks. For creating, writing, building.
MANAGER TIME    Hour-by-hour. For meetings, reviews, decisions.
RULE:           Be a maker in the morning, a manager in the afternoon.
```

### Seinfeld's "Don't break the chain"

Put a giant calendar on the wall. Every day you do your ONE Thing, draw a big red X. *"After a few days you'll have a chain. Your only job is to not break the chain."* (Reported by Brad Isaac, who got the advice from Jerry Seinfeld early in his career.)

## PART 4 — THE THREE COMMITMENTS (Chapter 16)

Time blocking only works if you make three commitments to your ONE Thing:

```
1. FOLLOW THE PATH OF MASTERY
   Mastery is a journey, not a destination.
   Always an apprentice for what's ahead, master of what's behind.
   The 10,000-hour pattern is real (Ericsson).
   It takes about 10 years at 4 hours/day to get there.

2. MOVE FROM "E" TO "P"
   Entrepreneurial (E) approach: do what comes naturally,
   hit a ceiling, give up, find greener pastures, repeat.
   Purposeful (P) approach: when natural ability hits a ceiling,
   find a better MODEL or SYSTEM and break through.
   "A different result requires doing something different."

3. LIVE THE ACCOUNTABILITY CYCLE
   Accountable people:
     Seek reality → Acknowledge it → Own it → Find solutions → Act.
   Victims:
     Avoid reality → Fight it → Blame others → Make excuses → Wait & hope.
   Get a COACH or accountability partner.
   Matthews' research: written goals + weekly progress to a friend
   = 76.7% achievement rate.
```

## PART 5 — THE FOUR THIEVES OF PRODUCTIVITY (Chapter 17)

Identify which thief is stealing the user's time, then call it by name:

```
THIEF #1 — INABILITY TO SAY "NO"
  One "yes" must be defended over time by 1,000 "nos."
  Steve Jobs at Apple 1997: cut the product line from 350 → 10.
  The THREE-FOOT RULE: a request must be connected to my ONE Thing
  to get within three feet of me. Otherwise: no, or redirect.

THIEF #2 — FEAR OF CHAOS
  Doing your ONE Thing well guarantees that other things go untended.
  This isn't failure — it's the cost of focus.
  Coppola: "Anything you build on a large scale or with intense
  passion invites chaos."
  Make peace with it.

THIEF #3 — POOR HEALTH HABITS
  Personal energy mismanagement is a silent thief of productivity.
  The book's daily energy plan:
    1. Meditate / pray             (spiritual energy)
    2. Eat right, exercise, sleep  (physical energy)
    3. Hug, kiss, laugh            (emotional energy)
    4. Set goals, plan, calendar   (mental energy)
    5. Time block your ONE Thing   (business energy)
  An 11-year UK civil servants study (n=7,100): 11+ hour workdays
  → 67% higher risk of heart disease.

THIEF #4 — ENVIRONMENT DOESN'T SUPPORT YOUR GOALS
  Christakis & Fowler: if a close friend becomes obese,
  you're 57% more likely to also become obese.
  Same effect for smoking, drinking, happiness — and ambition.
  Surround yourself with success-minded people.
  And design your physical space so your default path leads to
  your ONE Thing, not away from it.
```

## THE PARETO EXTREME

Pareto's 80/20 isn't enough — push it further. Each level of focus is a new 80/20:

```
100 things you could do
       ↓ 20%
   20 things that matter
       ↓ 20%
       4 that matter most
       ↓ 20%
       1 that matters most of all
```

> *"Don't get hung up on the numbers. Pareto's truth is about INEQUALITY. The ratio can play out as 80/20, 90/20, 70/10, 65/5 — but the principle is the same."*

## NO REGRETS

Bronnie Ware, palliative-care nurse, identified the top regrets of the dying. In order:

1. *I wish I'd had the courage to live a life true to myself, not the life others expected of me.* (#1 by a margin)
2. I wish I hadn't worked so hard.
3. I wish I'd had the courage to express my feelings.
4. I wish I'd stayed in touch with my friends.
5. I wish that I'd let myself be happier.

Gilovich & Medvec (1994): *"When people look back on their lives, it is the things they have NOT done that generate the greatest regret."* Inactions plague longer than actions.

**The connection to the ONE Thing:** living with purpose, priority, and productivity is the path away from these regrets.

## INTEGRATION WITH OTHER FRAMEWORKS

- **[[essentialism]] (McKeown)** — same core insight ("less but better"), different vocabulary. Essentialism is the *philosophy*; The ONE Thing is the *operating system*.
- **[[atomic-habits]] (Clear)** — pairs perfectly. Atomic Habits explains HOW to build the one habit; The ONE Thing helps you choose WHICH habit.
- **[[traction]] (Wickman)** — EOS Rocks are organisational ONE Things at the 90-day level. Use Traction for the team; The ONE Thing for the individual.
- **[[buy-back-your-time]] (Martell)** — once you know your ONE Thing, the buyback rate tells you what to delegate to protect time for it.
