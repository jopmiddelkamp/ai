## THE ONE LINE
Teamwork is rare. Most teams have dysfunction layered on dysfunction. Fix from the bottom.

## THE PYRAMID — COMPLETE

```
DYSFUNCTION 5: INATTENTION TO RESULTS
Team members pursue individual agendas over team goals.
Signs: People care about their department's numbers, not company numbers
       Political posturing. Status protection.
Fix: Public team scorecard. Collective goals. Shared accountability.

         ↑ Built on ↑

DYSFUNCTION 4: AVOIDANCE OF ACCOUNTABILITY
Nobody calls each other on bad behavior or poor performance.
Signs: Mediocrity is tolerated. Low performers coast.
       Leaders are the only ones who address issues.
Fix: Peer accountability. Make commitments explicit. Review them.

         ↑ Built on ↑

DYSFUNCTION 3: LACK OF COMMITMENT
Team members don't commit to decisions because they weren't heard.
Signs: Meetings end without clear decisions.
       People revisit decisions that were already made.
       Ambiguity about direction.
Fix: Force clarity. "We've decided X. Everyone agree to commit?"
     Disagree and commit culture.

         ↑ Built on ↑

DYSFUNCTION 2: FEAR OF CONFLICT
Teams avoid passionate debate about ideas.
Signs: Artificial harmony. Boring meetings.
       Real conversations happen outside the room.
       Decisions are made to avoid conflict, not to maximize results.
Fix: Mining for conflict (actively surface disagreements).
     Permission to disagree. No repercussions for honest debate.

         ↑ Built on ↑

DYSFUNCTION 1: ABSENCE OF TRUST (THE ROOT)
Team members are unwilling to be vulnerable with each other.
Signs: People protect themselves. Can't admit weaknesses or mistakes.
       Guard up in every meeting. No real connection.
Fix: Vulnerability-based trust exercises.
     Leader goes first. Always.
```

## THE TRUST-BUILDING EXERCISES

**PERSONAL HISTORIES EXERCISE (60 min):**
Each member shares:
→ Hometown, number of siblings, childhood challenge
→ First job, most difficult job
→ One thing unique about themselves that others might not know

Not therapy. Just enough to see each other as humans.
Studies show this dramatically increases empathy and trust.

**TEAM EFFECTIVENESS EXERCISE:**
Each member answers about each other:
→ "What is this person's greatest contribution to the team?"
→ "What area would most benefit their development?"

Shared openly. Non-defensive responses required.
Powerful and uncomfortable. Do it anyway.

**THE FEAR EXERCISE:**
Each member shares their single greatest fear about their work.
Vulnerability modeled by the leader first.

## THE CONFLICT CONTINUUM

```
Artificial           Healthy              Destructive
Harmony              Conflict             Fighting
    |─────────────────────|─────────────────|
   Bad              TARGET              Bad

Most teams are on the LEFT (avoiding conflict).
Some are on the RIGHT (personal attacks, no resolution).
Target: Middle (passionate debate about IDEAS, not people).

HOW TO MOVE LEFT TO MIDDLE:
→ Mining: Actively draw out perspectives
  "John, you seem to disagree. Tell us what you're thinking."
→ Real-time permission: "We're going to debate this. That's good."
→ No rescuing: When conflict happens, don't smooth it over prematurely.
→ Resolution: Always end with a decision, not just a discussion.
```
