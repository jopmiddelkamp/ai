## THE ONE LINE
Founder failures are predictable and preventable — most come from the Rich vs. King dilemma.

## THE RICH vs. KING FRAMEWORK

```
RICH: Maximize financial outcome
→ Take venture capital
→ Give up equity and control
→ Hire professional management
→ Build for acquisition or IPO
→ Accept being replaced as CEO if needed

KING: Maximize control and autonomy
→ Bootstrap or take limited capital
→ Keep equity and control
→ Stay as CEO regardless of company stage
→ Slower growth, more freedom
→ Define success on your own terms

MOST FOUNDERS: Want to be both Rich AND King
REALITY: These goals conflict as the company scales.

THE RESEARCH:
Founders with King motivation: Build smaller but retain control
Founders with Rich motivation: Build bigger but often lose the CEO role
Founders who are unclear: Make decisions that serve neither goal well

THE DECISION: Make this choice EARLY and consciously.
Then every subsequent decision aligns with your primary goal.

COFOUNDER CONFLICTS (most common startup killer):
→ Equity splits made too early (before knowing relative contribution)
→ Roles not clearly defined
→ Vesting schedules not established
→ Expectations not aligned

THE SOLUTIONS:
→ Equal equity ONLY if equal contribution over time (rare)
→ 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff (standard)
→ Clear roles with clear decision authority
→ Regular relationship check-ins (not just business)
```

## THE COFOUNDER AGREEMENT ESSENTIALS

```
BEFORE STARTING:
□ Equity split and rationale
□ Vesting schedule (4 years, 1 year cliff minimum)
□ Roles and decision authority
□ What happens if one cofounder leaves
□ Buyout mechanics
□ Non-compete agreement
□ IP assignment

DECISION AUTHORITY MATRIX:
Who can decide alone:
→ CEO: Daily operations, hiring below Director level
→ CTO: Technical architecture, engineering hiring
→ All cofounders: Major funding, acquisitions, equity grants, firing of leadership

What requires majority:
→ New strategic direction
→ Significant pivot
→ Major expense above threshold ($X)
```
