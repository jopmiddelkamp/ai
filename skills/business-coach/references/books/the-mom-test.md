## THE CUSTOMER INTERVIEW GUIDE

```
THE 45-MINUTE CUSTOMER DISCOVERY INTERVIEW:

OPENING (5 min):
→ "I'm not here to sell you anything. I'm learning."
→ "Tell me about your work/life in [relevant context]."
→ Let them talk. Don't interrupt.

ABOUT THEIR WORLD (15 min):
→ "What's the hardest part about [relevant problem area]?"
→ "Tell me about the last time that happened."
→ "Walk me through exactly what you did."
→ "How often does this happen?"
→ "What's that problem worth to you? [in time, money, frustration]"

ABOUT THEIR CURRENT SOLUTION (10 min):
→ "What have you tried to solve this?"
→ "How did that work out?"
→ "What's the cost of that solution?"
→ "What would make you switch from that?"

ABOUT YOUR IDEA (Don't pitch — probe) (10 min):
→ If you must mention your idea: "We're thinking about [vague description]. Does that seem relevant?"
→ If they show interest: "What would make this useful for you?"
→ If they offer to pay: "Can we actually work together?"

CLOSING (5 min):
→ "Who else should I talk to about this?"
→ "Is there anything I should have asked that I didn't?"
→ "Can I follow up with you as I learn more?"

RED FLAGS IN THE INTERVIEW:
→ They validate every idea (being polite, not honest)
→ They say "I would definitely use that" without specifics
→ They don't have any current workaround (problem isn't painful enough)
→ They ask when it will be ready (enthusiasm ≠ commitment to pay)

GREEN FLAGS IN THE INTERVIEW:
→ They describe a specific frustrating incident
→ They tell you how much they currently pay for a solution
→ They introduce you to colleagues with the same problem
→ They ask "how much will this cost?" and it's not to find a reason to say no
→ They try to pay you before you have a product
```

# BOOK 97: THE MOM TEST — ADVANCED APPLICATIONS

```
(Extended from Book 66 — Advanced customer development)

THE B2B CUSTOMER DISCOVERY ADDITIONS:

MAPPING THE BUYING JOURNEY:
Who initiates the search? (often not the buyer)
Who evaluates the options?
Who makes the final decision?
Who implements it?
Who lives with it daily?

All of these people have different needs.
You must understand all of them to sell effectively.

THE ECONOMIC BUYER vs. THE USER:
Economic buyer: Signs the check. Cares about ROI, risk, strategic fit.
User: Uses the product daily. Cares about features, ease, time saved.

Most companies sell to users (easier access).
Most deals are lost because economic buyers aren't convinced.

THE CHAMPION:
Someone inside the organization who sells for you internally.
They believe in you. They have credibility. They want you to win.

Your job in complex sales:
→ Find and enable your champion
→ Give them the information to sell internally
→ Make it easy for them to argue for you

WITHOUT A CHAMPION: Enterprise sales almost never close.
WITH A CHAMPION: Win rate improves dramatically.
```
