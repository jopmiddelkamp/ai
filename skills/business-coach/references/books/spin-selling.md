## THE ONE LINE
The techniques that close small sales — pressure, features, "ABC: Always Be Closing" — actively lose large sales. In major B2B, success comes from a specific sequence of questions, not from clever closes.

## THE CORE INSIGHT — SMALL SALES VS MAJOR SALES

```
                       SMALL SALE                MAJOR SALE
                       ───────────────           ───────────────────────
Selling cycle          Single call               Multi-call (months/years)
Decision maker         Individual                Multiple stakeholders
Customer's risk        Low (personal $)          High (career, reputation)
Post-sale relationship None                      Ongoing
Need development       Instant                   Slow, painful
Effective pressure?    Yes (closing works)       No (closing backfires)
Top behavior           Closing / Features        Questioning / Need development
```

**Decision rule:** If the sale takes more than one call, or if the buyer must justify the decision to others, you're in a major sale. Use SPIN.

## THE 4 STAGES OF EVERY SALES CALL

```
1. PRELIMINARIES         Opening, warming up
                         → ≤20% of call time. Don't over-invest.

2. INVESTIGATING         Asking questions to develop needs
                         → THIS is where you win the deal.

3. DEMONSTRATING CAPABILITY  Showing how your solution fits
                         → Only AFTER needs are developed.

4. OBTAINING COMMITMENT  Getting a clear next step
                         → Easy when investigating was done well.
```

Top performers spend most of their attention on stage 2. Average performers rush to stage 3.

## SPIN — THE 4 QUESTION TYPES

### S — Situation Questions
Background facts about the customer's current situation.

```
"What equipment are you using now?"
"How long have you had it?"
"How many users are on the system?"
```

**Research finding:** Negatively correlated with success. Inexperienced sellers ask too many.

**Rule:** Minimum needed. **Do homework first** — never ask what you could research. Every Situation Question must have a specific purpose.

> *"There's a special place in hell reserved for wicked salespeople where they sit for all eternity being forced to answer their own Situation Questions."*

### P — Problem Questions
Probe for problems, difficulties, dissatisfactions.

```
"Are you satisfied with your present equipment?"
"What disadvantages does this approach have?"
"Is reliability an issue?"
"Where does the current system slow your team down?"
```

**Research finding:** Foundational. Inexperienced sellers don't ask enough because they fear offending the customer.

**Rule:** Aim for 6+ Problem Questions per call. Plan 3+ problems your product can solve *before* the call.

### I — Implication Questions
Take a stated problem and explore its EFFECTS, CONSEQUENCES, RIPPLE.

```
"What effect does that have on output?"
"How much time does it cost your team weekly?"
"What's the cost of doing nothing?"
"Could this affect your competitiveness when [Competitor X] is moving on this?"
```

**Research finding:** The strongest predictor of success in major sales. Yet only ~1 in 20 questions in average sales calls is an Implication Question. Hard to ask spontaneously — must be PLANNED in advance.

**Purpose:** Take a problem the buyer perceives as small and build it large enough to justify expensive action.

> *"Even the smartest people we've studied find it hard to ask Implication Questions unless they've planned them in advance."*

### N — Need-Payoff Questions
Get the customer to articulate the VALUE of solving the problem — in their own words.

```
"How would solving this help you?"
"Why is that important to you?"
"Would it be useful if we could ___?"
"What other ways could this help your team?"
```

**Research finding:** Top performers ask far more Need-payoff Questions. Calls heavy in NPQs are rated by customers as POSITIVE, CONSTRUCTIVE, and HELPFUL.

**Two effects:**
1. Shifts attention from problem (negative) to solution (positive).
2. The customer tells YOU the benefits — far more persuasive than you telling them.

**Critical:** Do not ask NPQs about needs you cannot meet. You will only amplify a need your competitor can fill.

## QUINCY'S RULE — IMPLICATION VS NEED-PAYOFF

The hardest distinction in SPIN. An 8-year-old solved it instantly:

> **Implication Questions are always sad. Need-payoff Questions are always happy.**

```
IMPLICATION    Problem-centered  → SAD  (makes the problem bigger)
NEED-PAYOFF    Solution-centered → HAPPY (makes the solution attractive)
```

## THE SPIN LOGIC CHAIN

```
SITUATION (background) →  PROBLEM (find difficulty) →
                          ↓
                 IMPLIED NEED ("we have this problem")
                          ↓
                 IMPLICATION (make the problem bigger)
                          ↓
                 NEED-PAYOFF (build value of solving it)
                          ↓
                 EXPLICIT NEED ("we want / we need X")
                          ↓
                          BENEFIT ("we deliver X")
                          ↓
                          ADVANCE / ORDER
```

**SPIN is a guideline, not a script.** Top performers flex it. If the customer opens with an Explicit Need, jump straight to Need-payoff and Benefit.

## IMPLIED VS EXPLICIT NEEDS — THE KEY DISTINCTION

```
IMPLIED NEED    "Our system is unreliable."
                → Statement of a problem / dissatisfaction.
                → In small sales: predicts purchase.
                → In MAJOR sales: does NOT predict purchase.

EXPLICIT NEED   "We need a more reliable system."
                "We're going to do something about X."
                → Statement of want, intent, or desire.
                → In MAJOR sales: 2x more frequent in successful deals.
```

**The fundamental insight:** In major sales, success comes from converting Implied Needs into Explicit Needs — using Implication and Need-payoff Questions. That conversion is the entire job.

## FEATURES / ADVANTAGES / BENEFITS — REDEFINED

```
FEATURE        A fact about the product.
               Small sales: slightly positive.
               Major sales: NEUTRAL or NEGATIVE.

ADVANTAGE      How the product can be used / helps the customer.
               Small sales: positive.
               Major sales: SLIGHTLY positive early; decreases over the cycle.

BENEFIT        How the product meets an EXPLICIT NEED the customer expressed.
               Small sales: very positive.
               Major sales: VERY positive — throughout.
```

**The critical difference most sales training gets wrong:**

Wrong (Advantage dressed up as Benefit):
> *"Our system uses high-reliability components, so it'll solve your reliability problem."*
> — Reliability is still an Implied Need. This is an Advantage.

Right (true Benefit):
> Customer: *"We need a more reliable system."*
> Seller: *"Our system is built specifically for reliability and would meet that need directly."*

## CLOSING — THE COUNTERINTUITIVE TRUTH

Research findings from Rackham's 35,000-call study:

```
CONTEXT                              FINDING
─────────────────────────────────    ──────────────────────────────────────
Office equipment (190 calls)         High-close reps: 37% success
                                     Low-close reps: 70% success

Photo store — cheap goods            Closing improved: 72% → 76%

Photo store — expensive goods        Closing HURT: 42% → 33%

Customer satisfaction (n=145)        Closed customers: 5.8/10
                                     Non-closed: 7.7/10

Professional buyers (n=54)           34 of 54 said closing makes them
                                     LESS likely to buy
```

**Pressure rule:** Pressure helps with small decisions. Hurts with large ones.

**But zero closing also fails.** Even in major sales you must propose SOME commitment. The right number is *one* — a single, clear, realistic next step.

## OBTAINING COMMITMENT — WHAT TOP PERFORMERS DO

```
1. CONCENTRATE ATTENTION ON INVESTIGATING.
   "You don't need closing techniques with a customer who wants to buy."

2. CHECK THAT KEY CONCERNS ARE COVERED.
   "Before we go further, are there any areas you'd like me to tell
    you more about?"

3. SUMMARIZE THE BENEFITS.
   In long calls the customer loses track. Restate which Explicit
   Needs your offer meets.

4. PROPOSE A COMMITMENT — don't ask, tell.
   "The most logical next step would be for you and your CFO to
    come and see one of these systems in operation."
```

## ADVANCES VS CONTINUATIONS

In multi-call sales, the question is what defines a "successful" call.

```
ADVANCE        Some specific action that moves the deal forward.
               "I'll arrange the demo for next Tuesday at 2pm."
               "I'll introduce you to our CFO this week."

CONTINUATION   The call ended without an Advance.
               "It went really well. They want me to call back."
               → Looks positive. Often a polite delay.
```

**Most reps mistake Continuations for progress.** Top performers measure call success by Advances obtained, not by good feelings.

## OBJECTION HANDLING — UPSTREAM, NOT DOWNSTREAM

Traditional training: handle the objection when it comes up.
SPIN finding: objections are caused by HOW you sell, especially by jumping to Features and Advantages before the Explicit Need exists.

```
JUMP TO FEATURES too early       → PRICE objections
JUMP TO ADVANTAGES too early     → CAN-WE-REALLY-USE-THIS objections
DEVELOP EXPLICIT NEEDS first     → Almost no objections
```

The lesson: most objections aren't real signal. They're the cost of selling out of sequence.

## WHEN NOT TO USE SPIN

```
Transactional sub-$50 sales        → Use cheap closes; speed beats depth
Pure consumer impulse              → Emotional triggers > logical questioning
PLG self-serve product             → Buyer doesn't talk to sales until late
Single decision-maker, low-risk    → Small-sale toolkit works fine
```

## INTEGRATION WITH OTHER FRAMEWORKS

- **The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick)** — Both center on questioning past behavior, not predicted behavior. Mom Test for discovery; SPIN for sales.
- **The Challenger Sale** — A reframe is more useful AFTER SPIN has surfaced the right Implied Needs. Use SPIN to find the wound; Challenger to widen it.
- **$100M Offers (Hormozi)** — A grand-slam offer makes the Benefit stage near-effortless. SPIN tells you which Explicit Need to meet; $100M Offers tells you how to package it.

## CLAUDE PROMPT

```
Apply SPIN Selling to my deal.

The deal: [describe the customer + the situation]
Stage: [discovery / mid-cycle / late stage / stuck]
What's been said so far: [paraphrase last 1–2 conversations]
Stakeholders involved: [list]

Run the analysis:
1. Which 4 stages have we completed well, and which are skipped?
2. List the Implied Needs the customer has expressed so far.
3. Draft 3–5 Implication Questions I should plan for the next call
   to make the strongest problem bigger.
4. Draft 3–5 Need-payoff Questions for ONLY the problems I can solve.
5. What's the single Advance I should propose at the end of the next
   call (not a Continuation)?

Be honest if I'm running a small-sale playbook on a major sale.
```
