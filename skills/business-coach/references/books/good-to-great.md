# BOOK 03: GOOD TO GREAT
# Jim Collins
# ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## THE ONE LINE
Only 11 companies in history made the leap from good to great — and they all did it the same way.

## THE RESEARCH
Collins studied 1,435 companies over 40 years.
Found 11 that made the leap and sustained greatness.
Reverse-engineered what they had in common.
Everything in this book is empirically derived, not theory.

## THE 7 CHARACTERISTICS OF GREAT COMPANIES

**CHARACTERISTIC 1: LEVEL 5 LEADERSHIP**
(Covered deeply in ../frameworks/deep.md)
Paradox of personal humility + professional will.
Quiet, determined leaders — not charismatic celebrities.

Counterintuitive finding: The charismatic celebrity CEO often hurts long-term performance.
Why: They make the company dependent on their personal energy.
Level 5 leaders make the company outlast them.

**CHARACTERISTIC 2: FIRST WHO, THEN WHAT**
Get the right people on the bus BEFORE deciding where to drive.

"If we get the right people on the bus, the right people in the right seats, and the wrong people off the bus, then we'll figure out how to take it someplace great."

Why people first:
→ If you start with "what" and wrong people, strategy fails
→ Right people self-motivate, adapt when conditions change
→ You can pivot direction without changing your people

THE RIGOROUS STANDARDS (not ruthless):
When in doubt about a person: don't hire.
When you know you need to make a people change: act.
"Most leaders know within 6 months when something isn't working.
Most wait 3 years to do something about it."

**CHARACTERISTIC 3: CONFRONT THE BRUTAL FACTS**
(The Stockdale Paradox)
Create a culture where truth is heard.
Truth cannot reach the top if the messenger is shot.

4 practices for truth-telling culture:
1. Lead with questions, not answers
2. Engage in dialogue and debate, not coercion
3. Conduct autopsies without blame
4. Build "red flag" mechanisms (any employee can escalate)

**CHARACTERISTIC 4: THE HEDGEHOG CONCEPT**
(Covered deeply in ../frameworks/deep.md)
Find the intersection of passion, best-in-world, and economic engine.
Organize everything around it.

The key discipline: Saying NO to things that don't fit the Hedgehog.
Every company studied disciplined themselves to say NO far more than YES.

**CHARACTERISTIC 5: CULTURE OF DISCIPLINE**
Disciplined people → Don't need hierarchy
Disciplined thought → Don't need bureaucracy
Disciplined action → Don't need excessive controls

"Stop doing" list as important as "to do" list.
Collins found that great companies STOPPED doing as many things as they started.

THE STOP DOING LIST:
Every year: What are we currently doing that, if we knew then what we know now, we would not have started?
Make a list. Stop doing those things.
The cost of continuing is usually higher than the discomfort of stopping.

**CHARACTERISTIC 6: TECHNOLOGY ACCELERATORS**
Great companies are not pioneers of technology.
They become pioneers of the APPLICATION of technology
when it fits their Hedgehog.

"Technology is an accelerator of momentum, not a creator of it."

Question to ask about any technology investment:
"Does this technology fit directly with our Hedgehog Concept?"
If yes: Become a pioneer in applying it.
If no: Downgrade or ignore.

**CHARACTERISTIC 7: THE FLYWHEEL**
(Covered in ../frameworks/deep.md)
No single defining action.
No grand program.
No killer innovation.
Just consistent pushing in a consistent direction over time.

Outsiders see overnight success.
Insiders know they've been pushing the flywheel for years.

## THE KEY INSIGHT FOR TODAY'S ENTREPRENEUR
Good to Great companies didn't have better resources, better luck, or better markets.
They had better DISCIPLINE.
They were more rigorous about who they hired.
More honest about what wasn't working.
More focused on their core.
More patient with the flywheel.
