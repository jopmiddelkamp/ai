## THE ONE LINE
Most small businesses fail because they're started by technicians who don't know how to build a business.

## THE THREE PERSONALITIES

Every founder has three personalities in constant conflict:

**THE ENTREPRENEUR** (10% of the time):
Lives in the future. Dreams. Creates. Innovates.
"What if we did this? What about that market? We could be..."
The source of the vision. Also the source of chaos.

**THE MANAGER** (20% of the time):
Lives in the past. Plans. Organizes. Standardizes.
"We need a process for this. Who owns that? Where are the reports?"
The source of order. Also the source of rigidity.

**THE TECHNICIAN** (70% of the time for most founders):
Lives in the present. Does. Builds. Executes.
"I'll just do it myself. It's faster. They won't do it right."
The source of quality. Also the trap that prevents scaling.

THE CRISIS: Most businesses are started by Technicians
having an Entrepreneurial seizure.
They're great at baking pies. They open a bakery.
Now they have to do everything: bake, sell, market, manage, finance.
They're not great at any of those. The business fails.

OR: They DO handle all of it. Work 80 hours a week.
Build a job, not a business. The business doesn't run without them.

## THE FRANCHISE PROTOTYPE SOLUTION

The KEY mental shift: Build your business AS IF you're going to franchise it.

Questions to ask about everything:
"How would a new employee do this if I weren't here?"
"What documentation would they need?"
"What training would produce consistent results?"
"What checklist would catch the common mistakes?"

THE MCDONALD'S EXAMPLE:
McDonald's doesn't have the world's best burgers.
But they can open 38,000 locations with consistent quality.
Because they built systems, not reliance on talented people.

Your goal: "The best system in your industry."
Not: "The best person in your industry."

## THE BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT PROCESS

**INNOVATION**: Finding a better way.
Not dramatic breakthroughs. 1% improvements.
"What small improvement could we make to this step?"

**QUANTIFICATION**: Measuring the improvement.
If you can't measure it, you can't improve it.
"What's the baseline? What's the result?"

**ORCHESTRATION**: Systematizing the improvement.
"This improvement now becomes the new standard."

This loop, applied to every part of your business,
creates continuous compounding improvement.

## THE PRIMARY AIM

Before building your business model, define your Primary Aim.
The life you want to live. Not the business you want to build.

Questions:
→ What do you value most?
→ What kind of life do you want to have?
→ Who do you want to be in 10 years?

Then: Build a business that SUPPORTS that life.
Not the other way around.

Most entrepreneurs build a business and then try to fit their
life around it. Gerber says: Start with the life. Build the
business to serve it.
