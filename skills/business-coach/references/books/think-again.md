## THE ARMCHAIR QUARTERBACK PROBLEM

We form strong opinions on things we know little about.
The more confident someone sounds: the less they may actually know.
(Dunning-Kruger at scale)

THE SOLUTION: Track your predictions.
Write down your confident beliefs. Date them.
Come back in 6 months. Check the record.
Nothing cures overconfidence like a recorded history of being wrong.

## THE SCIENCE OF CHANGING MINDS

Grant's research: The most effective persuasion is self-persuasion.
You can't convince anyone of anything.
You can create the conditions for them to convince themselves.

THE MOTIVATIONAL INTERVIEWING SEQUENCE:
1. Ask about their experience
2. Acknowledge it genuinely
3. Ask what they value about their current position
4. Ask if there are any circumstances where they might see it differently
5. Ask what they would need to see to update their view

WHAT DOESN'T WORK:
→ More facts (they dig in deeper)
→ Emotional appeals (activates defensiveness)
→ Attacking their position (they protect it)

WHAT WORKS:
→ Genuine curiosity about their reasoning
→ Finding common ground first
→ Helping them identify inconsistencies in their own thinking
