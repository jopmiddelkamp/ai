## THE ONE LINE
The supreme art of war is to subdue the enemy without fighting — applied to business: win markets without direct competition.

## THE 13 CHAPTERS — BUSINESS APPLIED

```
1. LAYING PLANS
Know yourself. Know your enemy. Know the terrain.
→ Assess your strengths and weaknesses honestly
→ Assess your competitor's strengths and weaknesses
→ Assess the market conditions (terrain)
→ Only then: Make plans

"If you know the enemy and know yourself,
you need not fear the result of a hundred battles."

2. WAGING WAR
Prolonged war is expensive. Win quickly or don't fight.
→ Don't enter markets you can't win fast
→ Speed beats perfection in competitive situations
→ The cost of prolonged competition destroys both combatants

3. ATTACK BY STRATAGEM
Win without fighting. Outsmart, don't outmuscle.
→ Best strategy: Attack where they're not (Blue Ocean)
→ Next best: Break their alliances/partnerships
→ Next: Attack their strategy
→ Worst: Direct assault on their strength

4. TACTICAL DISPOSITIONS
First make yourself undefeatable. Then wait for opportunity.
→ Build your defensive position first
→ Create a business that can't be easily beaten
→ Don't attack until you see clear advantage

5. ENERGY (Momentum)
Use momentum like a drawn crossbow. Release at the right moment.
→ Build up. Then attack decisively.
→ Don't charge in small increments. Build, then strike fully.

6. WEAK POINTS AND STRONG POINTS
Attack where they're weak. Avoid where they're strong.
→ Map your competitor's weak points (underserved customers, poor service, geography)
→ Build there before they do

7. MANEUVERING
The most direct route is often not the fastest.
→ Indirect approaches often win (flank, don't charge frontally)
→ Move where they least expect you

8. VARIATION IN TACTICS
Adapt to circumstances. Never use the same approach twice.
→ Rigid strategy is vulnerable strategy
→ The flexible and adaptive outlasts the powerful and rigid

9-13. REMAINING CHAPTERS:
Intelligence, terrain, fire attacks, use of spies.
Business translation: Market research, competitive intelligence,
                      knowing your market deeply.
```

## THE 5 FACTORS OF STRATEGY

```
1. THE TAO (Moral Law): Your team believes in what you're doing
2. HEAVEN: Timing (market conditions, economic environment)
3. EARTH: Terrain (market landscape, geography)
4. THE COMMANDER: Leadership (skills, integrity, wisdom)
5. METHOD AND DISCIPLINE: Execution systems

ALL 5 MUST ALIGN for strategic success.
Missing any one: Vulnerable to failure.
```
