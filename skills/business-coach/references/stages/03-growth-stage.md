# 🚀 STAGE 03: GROWTH STAGE
### From $1M to $10M — The Valley of Death
*The stage where most businesses stall forever*

---

## WHY THIS STAGE IS DIFFERENT

```
EARLY STAGE ($0-$1M):
Founder energy drives everything.
Personal hustle can cover any gap.
Systems don't matter yet.

GROWTH STAGE ($1M-$10M):
Personal hustle hits a wall.
What got you here won't get you there.
Systems, people, and capital determine the ceiling.

SCALING STAGE ($10M+):
Professional management required.
Systems run themselves.
Founder role completely changes.

THE VALLEY OF DEATH PROBLEM:
At $1M: You're profitable but everything runs through you.
At $3M: You've hired people but they can't execute without you.
At $5M: You're paying for a team that isn't delivering.
At $7M: You realize you need a completely different business.
At $10M: You either rebuilt it or you're stuck at $5-7M forever.

The businesses that cross $10M share one thing:
They rebuilt their operating system BEFORE they needed to.
```

## THE 7 STRATEGIC MOVES FROM $1M TO $10M

### MOVE 1: HIRE YOUR INTEGRATOR
**Source: Traction — Gino Wickman**

```
THE VISIONARY/INTEGRATOR DYNAMIC:
Most founders are Visionaries:
→ Creative, strategic, inspirational
→ Hate details, follow-through, accountability
→ Great at starting, poor at finishing

What they need: An Integrator
→ Executes the Visionary's ideas
→ Holds the team accountable
→ Manages the P&L
→ Loves details and process
→ Great at finishing, less creative at starting

WITHOUT AN INTEGRATOR:
The Visionary spins out new ideas that never get executed.
The team is confused by constantly changing priorities.
Revenue plateaus at whatever the Visionary can personally manage.

HOW TO FIND YOUR INTEGRATOR:
→ Look internally first (who already acts like one?)
→ Look for COO, General Manager, Director of Operations
→ Profile: DISC profile = high C/S (not high D like most founders)
→ References should say: "Gets things done. Holds people accountable."
→ Green flag: They find your chaos exhausting (not exciting)

COMPENSATION: Typically 60-80% of founder cash comp.
Equity: 5-15% for the right person.

THE MOST IMPORTANT HIRE YOU'LL EVER MAKE.
```

### MOVE 2: INSTALL YOUR OPERATING SYSTEM
**Source: Traction + Scaling Up — Wickman + Harnish**

```
THE 3 GROWTH STAGE OS OPTIONS:

EOS (Entrepreneurial Operating System):
Best for: 10-150 person companies
Tools: VTO, Rocks, L10 meetings, Scorecard, Accountability Chart
Investment: $0 (DIY) to $15K/year (EOS Implementer)
Time to install: 6-12 months

SCALING UP (Rockefeller Habits):
Best for: 50-500 person companies
Tools: One-Page Strategic Plan, Meeting rhythms, KPIs, OKRs
More sophisticated than EOS
Investment: Books + coaching

OKRs (Google's system):
Best for: Tech/product companies
Objectives + Key Results
Quarterly cadence
Best documentation: Measure What Matters (John Doerr)

START WITH EOS. You can graduate to Scaling Up at $10M+.

THE MINIMUM VIABLE OS (for $1M-$3M):
1. Annual planning meeting (1-2 days offsite)
2. Quarterly planning meeting (1 day)
3. Weekly L10 meeting (90 min, non-negotiable)
4. Quarterly Rocks (3-7 per leader)
5. Weekly scorecard (5-10 numbers)
6. Accountability chart (who owns what)

With just these 6 things: Clarity increases 80%.
```

### MOVE 3: BUILD YOUR MANAGEMENT LAYER
**Source: The Five Dysfunctions + Multipliers**

```
THE MANAGEMENT GAP:
At $1M: Founder manages everyone directly.
At $3M: Too many people to manage directly.
At $5M: Managers are needed but often promoted wrong.

THE WRONG WAY TO BUILD MANAGEMENT:
Promote your best performers into management.
(Your best salesperson → Sales Manager)
→ You lose your best performer
→ You get a bad manager
→ The role requires completely different skills

THE RIGHT WAY:
→ Hire experienced managers from outside
→ OR: Promote people who show MANAGEMENT behaviors
   (not performance behaviors)

MANAGER BEHAVIORS (look for these):
→ Naturally coaches others
→ Gets more excited by team wins than personal wins
→ Thinks about how to make others successful
→ Calm and organized, not chaotic and creative
→ Keeps commitments obsessively

THE MANAGER'S JOB (radical simplification):
1. Set clear expectations
2. Hire people who can meet them
3. Remove obstacles
4. Hold people accountable
5. Develop people's skills

THAT'S IT. If they're doing those 5 things: great manager.
If not: coach or replace.

THE MANAGEMENT MEETING STRUCTURE:
1-on-1s: Weekly, 30 min, focused on the PERSON not the work
Team meetings: Weekly, focused on the WORK not the person
Skip-levels: Quarterly, leader talks to reports' reports
```

### MOVE 4: FIX YOUR UNIT ECONOMICS
**Source: Profit First + The Psychology of Money**

```
THE GROWTH STAGE FINANCIAL CRISIS:
Counterintuitively: Revenue growth often DESTROYS margin.
Because: Costs grow faster than revenue during scaling.

THE 4 NUMBERS EVERY GROWTH STAGE COMPANY MUST TRACK:

1. CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
   Total sales + marketing spend ÷ new customers acquired
   Benchmark: CAC < 33% of LTV

2. LTV (Customer Lifetime Value)
   Average revenue per customer × gross margin × retention
   Your goal: LTV/CAC ratio > 3:1

3. GROSS MARGIN
   (Revenue - COGS) ÷ Revenue
   Software: Should be 70%+
   Services: Should be 40-60%
   Products: Should be 30-50%

4. CASH CONVERSION CYCLE
   Days to convert investment into cash
   Lower is always better

THE GROSS MARGIN SQUEEZE:
Growth stage companies often see margins DECLINE.
Why: Hiring ahead of revenue, serving new customers less efficiently.
Fix: Don't grow revenue until margins are healthy.

THE RULE: Never grow top line faster than you can maintain margins.
"Revenue solves everything" is a lie.
Revenue with bad margins accelerates your problems.

PROFIT FIRST IN GROWTH STAGE:
Recommended allocations at $1M-$5M:
→ Profit: 10-15%
→ Owner's Pay: 15-25%
→ Tax: 15-20%
→ Operating Expenses: 45-55%

If OpEx is above 60%: You have a cost problem.
```

### MOVE 5: CHOOSE ONE GROWTH CHANNEL
**Source: $100M Leads + Crossing the Chasm**

```
THE CHANNEL TRAP:
Growth stage companies try to do everything at once.
→ Social media + paid ads + SEO + partnerships + sales team
→ None get enough investment to work
→ None get properly measured
→ Everything underperforms
→ "Marketing doesn't work for us"

THE FOCUS RULE:
Pick ONE primary acquisition channel.
Master it completely.
Only add a second channel when the first is running without you.

THE CHANNEL OPTIONS (pick one):
1. CONTENT MARKETING
   Best for: Longer sales cycles, educating buyers
   Metrics: Traffic, leads, cost per lead
   Timeline: 6-18 months to meaningful results

2. PAID ACQUISITION
   Best for: Proven offer, known target customer
   Metrics: CAC, ROAS, conversion rate
   Timeline: 2-4 weeks to test, 2-3 months to optimize

3. OUTBOUND SALES
   Best for: High-ticket B2B, defined target list
   Metrics: Connect rate, meeting rate, close rate
   Timeline: 30-90 days to pipeline

4. PARTNERSHIPS/REFERRALS
   Best for: Known industries with referral networks
   Metrics: Partners enrolled, referrals per partner
   Timeline: 3-6 months to activate

5. COMMUNITY/EVENTS
   Best for: Industries where relationships drive deals
   Metrics: Events attended, meetings booked, deals influenced
   Timeline: 6-12 months to build relationships

THE CROSSING THE CHASM INSIGHT:
Early adopters and mainstream market want different things.
Don't try to serve both simultaneously.
Dominate a niche completely. Then expand.
"Be a big fish in a small pond before becoming a big fish in a big pond."
```

### MOVE 6: SYSTEMIZE YOUR DELIVERY
**Source: E-Myth + Traction**

```
THE DELIVERY CRISIS:
Early stage: Founder personally delivers. Quality is high.
Growth stage: Team delivers. Quality is inconsistent.
Result: Customer complaints, churn, reputation damage.

THE CONSISTENCY FRAMEWORK:
Step 1: Define your quality standard (what does "done right" look like?)
Step 2: Map the delivery process (every step from signed to delivered)
Step 3: Identify the failure points (where does quality slip?)
Step 4: Build checklists and standards for each failure point
Step 5: Train to standard, not to intuition
Step 6: Measure and report quality weekly

THE CHECKLIST MANIFESTO INSIGHT:
(Atul Gawande — not on our list but principle used everywhere)
Experts fail not from lack of knowledge but from failure to apply it.
Checklists catch the simple errors that expertise makes us skip.
→ Use checklists for everything repeatable.

CUSTOMER SUCCESS METRICS:
NPS (Net Promoter Score): "0-10, how likely to recommend us?"
CSAT: Customer satisfaction on specific interactions
Churn rate: % of customers who leave per month
Expansion revenue: % of revenue from existing customers growing

TARGET BENCHMARKS:
NPS: Above 50 is excellent, 70+ is world class
Monthly churn: Below 2% for services, below 5% for SaaS
Expansion: Aim for expansion > churn (negative churn)
```

### MOVE 7: BUILD YOUR LEADERSHIP TEAM
**Source: Good to Great + No Rules Rules (Hastings)**

```
THE $5M INFLECTION POINT:
Under $5M: Founder + key individual contributors
Over $5M: Need functional leaders who own their domains

THE ROLES TO FILL (in order of priority):
1. Operations/Integrator (if not yet hired)
2. Sales leader (if sales is your bottleneck)
3. Finance (if you don't have clear financial visibility)
4. People/HR (at 20+ employees)
5. Marketing leader (when paid channels are primary)

NETFLIX CULTURE INSIGHTS (No Rules Rules):
→ Hire only exceptional people (even if it means smaller team)
→ Pay top of market (don't lose A players to save 20%)
→ Remove good performers: "If this person told me they were leaving,
   would I fight to keep them? If no, pay them severance now."
→ No performance improvement plans: "If you need a PIP, you're
   in the wrong seat."

THE A/B/C PLAYER FRAMEWORK:
A players: Do the work of 3 people. Elevate everyone around them.
B players: Solid performers. Hold the business steady.
C players: Underperform. Cost more than they contribute.

Growth stage companies can afford a few B players.
They cannot afford any C players.

THE LEADERSHIP TEAM MEETING (Monthly):
2-4 hours. Focused on:
→ Metrics review (is the business on track?)
→ Strategic issues (what problems need leader-level attention?)
→ Cross-functional decisions (where do departments interact?)
→ 90-day Rock review (are we executing our commitments?)
NOT: Operations updates (those go in their department meetings)
```
