# 🌱 STAGE 01: IDEA STAGE
### From Nothing to Validated Concept
*Before you build anything, read this.*

---

## THE CARDINAL RULE OF THIS STAGE

**Do not build anything until someone pays you.**

The biggest mistake entrepreneurs make is spending months building something nobody wants. The frameworks in this section exist to prevent that mistake.

---

## THE STAGE CHECKLIST

```
□ I can describe my customer in 1 specific sentence
□ I have talked to 20+ real potential customers (not friends)
□ At least 3 people have tried to pay me (or pre-paid)
□ I know the #1 pain I solve and can prove it with customer quotes
□ I know who my competitors are and why I'm different
□ I have a simple, ugly version of my offer ready to test
□ I know how I'll make money (business model)
```

## FRAMEWORK 1: THE MOM TEST
**Source: The Mom Test**

Before you talk to customers, learn to ask the right questions.

```
THE WRONG WAY (what most people do):
"Would you use this app?"      → They say yes to be nice
"What do you think of my idea?" → They give vague feedback
"Would you pay for this?"       → They lie

THE RIGHT WAY (Mom Test):
"What's the hardest part about [their problem]?"
"Tell me about the last time that happened."
"What have you already tried?"
"How much does this cost you now?"
"Who else should I talk to?"

THE SIGNAL YOU'RE LOOKING FOR:
🟢 They describe the problem vividly without prompting
🟢 They've already tried to solve it (proves it's real)
🟢 They ask when they can buy
🟢 They offer to introduce you to others who have the problem
🔴 They say "interesting" or "that's cool"
🔴 They give generic positive feedback
🔴 They're enthusiastic but vague
```

**Claude Prompt:**
```
Help me prepare for customer discovery interviews using The Mom Test.
My business idea: [describe]
My assumed customer: [describe]
My assumed problem: [describe]

Write me:
1. 10 Mom Test-approved interview questions
2. Questions I should NEVER ask (and why)
3. What to look for as signals of real pain
4. A script for the first 2 minutes of the call
```

## FRAMEWORK 2: ZERO TO ONE THINKING
**Source: Zero to One — Peter Thiel**

```
THE CORE QUESTION: Are you creating something new (0→1) 
or copying what exists (1→n)?

0→1: Creates new value. Hard. Rewarding. Defensible.
1→n: Incremental improvement. Easy. Competitive. Commoditized.

THE 7 QUESTIONS EVERY STARTUP MUST ANSWER:
① The Engineering Question: Can you create breakthrough technology?
② The Timing Question: Is now the right time to start?
③ The Monopoly Question: Are you starting with a big share of a small market?
④ The People Question: Do you have the right team?
⑤ The Distribution Question: Can you deliver your product?
⑥ The Durability Question: Will your position be defensible in 10-20 years?
⑦ The Secret Question: Have you identified a unique opportunity others don't see?

THE MONOPOLY FRAMEWORK:
Don't compete. Dominate a small market first.
Then expand outward.

Amazon: Books → Everything
Facebook: Harvard → Colleges → Everyone
PayPal: eBay PowerSellers → Everyone

START SMALL ENOUGH TO WIN.
Then expand.
```

## FRAMEWORK 3: THE LEAN STARTUP
**Source: The Lean Startup — Eric Ries**

```
THE BUILD-MEASURE-LEARN LOOP:

         ┌─────────────────┐
         │     IDEAS        │
         └────────┬─────────┘
                  │
         ┌────────▼─────────┐
         │      BUILD        │  ← Build minimum viable product
         └────────┬─────────┘
                  │
         ┌────────▼─────────┐
         │     PRODUCT       │
         └────────┬─────────┘
                  │
         ┌────────▼─────────┐
         │     MEASURE       │  ← Measure real customer behavior
         └────────┬─────────┘
                  │
         ┌────────▼─────────┐
         │      DATA         │
         └────────┬─────────┘
                  │
         ┌────────▼─────────┐
         │      LEARN        │  ← Learn: pivot or persevere?
         └────────┬─────────┘
                  │
                  └─────────────► Back to IDEAS

THE MVP RULE:
Build the smallest thing that lets you learn.
NOT: smallest thing that's embarrassing to show
BUT: smallest thing that ANSWERS YOUR CORE QUESTION

THE PIVOT vs. PERSEVERE DECISION:
Are your core hypotheses validated? 
YES → Persevere (optimize)
NO → Pivot (change core assumption)
UNKNOWN → You need more data
```

## THE IDEA STAGE 30-DAY PLAN

```
WEEK 1: CUSTOMER DISCOVERY
• Talk to 20 potential customers (not friends, not family)
• Use Mom Test questions exclusively
• Take notes on exact words they use
• Look for patterns in the pain

WEEK 2: VALIDATE THE PROBLEM
• Synthesize your interview notes
• Can you describe the problem in their words?
• Is the problem common enough? (how many people have it?)
• Is it painful enough? (what's the cost of NOT solving it?)
• Can they pay? (what's their budget?)

WEEK 3: BUILD AN MVP
• What's the smallest version you can sell this week?
• Does NOT need to be software
• Could be: Google Doc, manual process, spreadsheet, PDF
• The question: Will people pay for this?

WEEK 4: GET YOUR FIRST SALE
• Offer it to 5 people for a discount (founding member price)
• Goal: At least 1 paid customer
• If nobody pays: You have a product problem, not a marketing problem
• If someone pays: You have validation. Now build.
```

## COMMON IDEA STAGE MISTAKES

```
❌ MISTAKE 1: BUILDING BEFORE VALIDATING
Fix: Don't write a line of code until someone pre-pays.

❌ MISTAKE 2: ASKING FRIENDS AND FAMILY
Fix: They will lie to protect your feelings. Find strangers.

❌ MISTAKE 3: SOLVING A PROBLEM YOU HAVE
(Can be good, but...)
Fix: Confirm others have the SAME problem before investing.

❌ MISTAKE 4: FALLING IN LOVE WITH THE SOLUTION
Fix: Fall in love with the problem. Solutions change.

❌ MISTAKE 5: WAITING FOR PERFECT
Fix: A bad product that ships beats a perfect product that doesn't.

❌ MISTAKE 6: NOT CHARGING ENOUGH EARLY
Fix: Charge full price from day 1. Discounts confuse your market.

❌ MISTAKE 7: TRYING TO SERVE EVERYONE
Fix: One specific customer. One specific problem. One specific solution.
```

## MASTER DIAGNOSTIC PROMPT FOR IDEA STAGE

```
I have a business idea and I'm at the idea stage. Act as a 
rigorous business advisor who has seen thousands of startups 
succeed and fail. Don't validate my idea — challenge it.

My idea: [describe]
My target customer: [describe]
The problem I'm solving: [describe]
Why I think this is a good idea: [describe]
What I've done so far: [describe]

Apply Thiel's 7 Questions and The Lean Startup framework:
1. What are the 3 biggest assumptions I'm making?
2. How would I test each assumption for under $500?
3. Who are my real competitors (including the "do nothing" option)?
4. What's my unfair advantage here?
5. What would have to be true for this to be a $1M business?
6. What's the smallest MVP I could build this week?
7. What's my 30-day validation plan?

Be honest. If this idea has fundamental problems, tell me.
```
