# PROBLEM: MY TEAM HAS BAD CULTURE

## DIAGNOSIS
```
Signs you have a culture problem:
□ People talk behind each other's backs
□ Meetings are political, not productive
□ Top performers are leaving
□ Mediocrity is tolerated
□ Decisions are made for appearance, not results
□ Values on the wall mean nothing in practice
□ The leader's behavior contradicts stated values
```

## THE FRAMEWORK STACK

**STEP 1: DIAGNOSE THE DYSFUNCTION LEVEL (Lencioni)**
```
Level 1 (Trust): Can people admit mistakes? Show vulnerability?
Level 2 (Conflict): Is real debate happening? Or artificial harmony?
Level 3 (Commitment): Are decisions clear? Does everyone commit?
Level 4 (Accountability): Do peers hold each other accountable?
Level 5 (Results): Does the team care about collective results?

Fix from the bottom. You cannot fix Level 5 while Level 1 is broken.
```

**STEP 2: AUDIT YOUR OWN BEHAVIOR (Culture Code + Extreme Ownership)**
```
90% of culture problems trace to leadership behavior.
Questions to ask honestly:
→ Do I admit mistakes publicly?
→ Do I give credit to others?
→ Do I ask questions more than give answers?
→ Do I tolerate bad behavior from high performers?
→ Do I fire people who violate values (even high performers)?

If the answer to any is "no" → You are the culture problem.
```

**STEP 3: INSTALL THE CULTURE CODE PRACTICES**
```
SAFETY: Make it safe to tell the truth.
→ React to bad news with curiosity, not punishment.
→ "Tell me more" not "who's responsible?"

VULNERABILITY: Model it yourself.
→ Share a recent failure in the next team meeting.
→ Ask for help with something you don't know.

PURPOSE: Connect to meaning.
→ Tell stories that embody your values.
→ Recognize people for value-aligned behavior (not just results).
```

**CLAUDE PROMPT:**
```
My team has culture problems. Help me diagnose and fix them.

Symptoms I'm seeing: [describe]
Team size: [number]
How long this has been a problem: [timeframe]
My honest self-assessment as a leader: [describe]

Apply Lencioni, Culture Code, and Extreme Ownership.
Start by telling me what I might be doing wrong.
Then give me a 30-day culture reset plan.
```

# PROBLEM: I'M LOSING MOTIVATION

## DIAGNOSIS
```
Types of motivation loss:
A. BURNOUT: Exhausted from overwork
B. MEANINGLESSNESS: Disconnected from why it matters
C. STUCK: Not making progress despite effort
D. WRONG PATH: Doing something you never actually wanted
```

## THE FRAMEWORK STACK

**IF BURNOUT (type A):**
```
From Buy Back Your Time + The 5 Types of Wealth:

IMMEDIATE:
→ Cancel the 20% lowest-value commitments this week
→ Add one non-negotiable recovery activity daily
→ Sleep is first, not optional

MEDIUM TERM:
→ Run the time and energy audit
→ Delegate everything below your buyback rate
→ Restructure to fewer things done better

LONGER TERM:
→ Build systems that remove you from operational roles
→ You're burned out because you're doing what systems should do
```

**IF MEANINGLESSNESS (type B):**
```
From Man's Search for Meaning + Start With Why + The Infinite Game:

THE MEANING RECOVERY QUESTIONS:
→ Why did you start this in the first place?
→ Who benefits from your work beyond yourself?
→ What would be lost if your business didn't exist?
→ What would you do if you knew it would work?

THE REFRAME:
Revenue is the score. The game is the impact.
Reconnect to the impact. The revenue becomes the by-product.
```

**IF STUCK (type C):**
```
From The Obstacle is the Way + Can't Hurt Me + Grit:

The feeling of being stuck = you're at the edge of your capability.
This is where growth lives. The discomfort is the signal.

→ The 40% rule: You're operating at 40% of capacity.
→ Break the task into smaller experiments.
→ Find the smallest version that would feel like progress.
→ Take that step today.
```

**IF WRONG PATH (type D):**
```
From The Alchemist + Die Empty + Principles:

This is the hardest one to face.
Most people never face it. They live someone else's life.

THE BRUTAL HONEST QUESTIONS:
→ If you had $10M in the bank, would you still do this?
→ Are you pursuing this for YOU or for someone else's approval?
→ What would you build if nobody was watching?

If the answers point to "wrong path":
→ This is information, not a crisis.
→ Build your exit strategy while running today's business.
→ The transition takes time. Start it now.
```

# PROBLEM: COMPETITION IS WINNING

## DIAGNOSIS
```
Types of competitive threats:
A. PRICE COMPETITION: They're cheaper
B. PRODUCT COMPETITION: They have better features
C. MARKETING COMPETITION: They have more visibility
D. DISRUPTIVE THREAT: They're building a different model
```

## THE FRAMEWORK STACK

**IF PRICE COMPETITION:**
```
From Blue Ocean + $100M Offers + Playing to Win:

NEVER COMPETE ON PRICE. Here's why:
→ Race to bottom — both lose
→ Price-sensitive customers are the worst customers
→ You'll always lose to someone more desperate

INSTEAD:
→ Move upmarket (add value, raise prices)
→ Find a segment that values something other than price
→ Build such strong differentiation that price comparison is irrelevant

The question: "What can I offer that's 10x more valuable than the cheapest alternative?"
```

**IF PRODUCT COMPETITION:**
```
From Good to Great + Zero to One + Innovator's Dilemma:

Are they 10% better or 10x better?
10% better: Marketing and relationships can overcome this.
10x better: You have a real problem.

If 10x behind: Stop improving incrementally.
Find the category of performance where YOU can be 10x better.
You don't need to win on their terms. Change the terms.
```

**IF MARKETING COMPETITION:**
```
From Contagious + StoryBrand + Influence:

Being outspent on marketing ≠ losing.
The best marketing is word-of-mouth + story + community.

→ STEPPS: Is your content/product designed to spread?
→ StoryBrand: Is your messaging clear and customer-centric?
→ Unity: Are you building community, not just customers?
→ Social Currency: Do customers get social status from using you?
```

**IF DISRUPTIVE THREAT:**
```
From Innovator's Dilemma + Blue Ocean:

Take it seriously. Most incumbents don't.
The ones that do: Usually survive.
The ones that don't: Die. Every time.

RESPONSE OPTIONS:
→ Create a separate entity to compete with the disrupter
→ Acquire them
→ Move upmarket (where disruption hasn't reached yet)
→ Out-disrupt them (disrupt yourself first)
```

# PROBLEM: I CAN'T SCALE

## THE SCALING FRAMEWORK

```
SCALING BOTTLENECKS (find yours):
□ People: Not enough people or wrong people
□ Systems: No documented processes, tribal knowledge
□ Capital: Running out of money before revenue scales
□ Market: Addressable market is too small
□ Product: Product breaks at scale
□ Founder: You are the bottleneck (see founder-bottleneck.md)

THE DIAGNOSTIC:
"What would prevent me from doubling revenue in 12 months?"
Whatever your first answer is: That's your scaling bottleneck.
Focus there exclusively.
```

**THE VERNE HARNISH 4 DECISIONS:**
```
PEOPLE: Do you have the right people in the right roles?
Scale requires A-players in every leadership seat.
One wrong person in a key seat can block all scaling.

STRATEGY: Is your differentiation clear?
You can't scale what doesn't have a clear reason to exist.
Who is this for? Why you over alternatives?

EXECUTION: Do you have the operating rhythms?
Scaling requires systems that run without you.
EOS or equivalent: Non-negotiable.

CASH: Do you have the capital to fuel growth?
Revenue scaling requires working capital.
Understanding and managing cash conversion cycle = survival.

FIX THE WEAKEST OF THE 4 FIRST.
All 4 must be strong to sustain scaling.
```

# PROBLEM: MONEY IS NOT WORKING

## DIAGNOSIS
```
Financial problems in business (identify yours):
A. Revenue too low
B. Costs too high relative to revenue (margin problem)
C. Cash flow timing (revenue and expenses don't align)
D. Profit exists but cash doesn't (working capital problem)
E. Growth requires more capital than you have
```

**FOR EACH PROBLEM TYPE:**

**A. REVENUE TOO LOW → See cant-get-customers.md**

**B. MARGIN PROBLEM:**
```
FROM PROFIT FIRST + GOOD STRATEGY BAD STRATEGY:

AUDIT YOUR COSTS:
Fixed costs: Rent, salaries, software subscriptions, insurance
Variable costs: COGS, contractors, commissions

THE GROSS MARGIN TEST BY INDUSTRY:
Software: Should be 70%+
Professional services: Should be 50-70%
Physical products: Should be 30-50%
Restaurants: Should be 60-70% (food cost 30-40%)

If your gross margin is below industry standard:
→ Your pricing is wrong, OR
→ Your delivery costs are too high, OR
→ You're serving the wrong customers (ones who need too much)

THE FIX: Raise prices + Productize delivery (reduce custom work)
```

**C. CASH FLOW TIMING:**
```
CASH CONVERSION CYCLE:
Days to collect from customers - Days to pay suppliers

Shorten it by:
→ Require deposits or upfront payment
→ Invoice immediately upon completion (not end of month)
→ Offer early payment discounts
→ Negotiate extended payment terms with suppliers

NET 30 CLIENTS: Are they worth it?
Often no. The carrying cost of slow payment is expensive.
Consider a late payment fee: 1.5% per month.
Many clients will pay faster simply to avoid the fee.
```

**D. WORKING CAPITAL PROBLEM:**
```
Revenue is there. Profit is there. But cash isn't.

CAUSES:
→ Inventory: Buying before you sell
→ Receivables: Selling before you collect
→ Growth: Hiring/investing ahead of revenue

SOLUTIONS:
→ Line of credit for working capital (not for operations)
→ Invoice factoring (sell receivables at a discount)
→ Require deposits from clients
→ Pre-sell the product before buying inventory
```

# PROBLEM: WRONG PEOPLE ON MY TEAM

## THE PEOPLE AUDIT FRAMEWORK

```
FROM TRACTION + GOOD TO GREAT + NETFLIX:

STEP 1: THE KEEPER TEST (Netflix)
For each person on your team:
"If they told me tomorrow they were leaving for a competitor,
how hard would I fight to keep them?"

Fight hard → Keep
Mixed feelings → Develop (with specific plan and timeline)
Relief → Transition out (you already know)

STEP 2: THE PEOPLE ANALYZER (EOS)
Rate each person on each core value: + / +/- / -
Then GWC the role: Get it? Want it? Capacity?

Right Person + Right Seat = Keep and grow
Right Person + Wrong Seat = Move within organization
Wrong Person + Right Seat = Help them find right organization
Wrong Person + Wrong Seat = Transition immediately

STEP 3: THE CULTURAL VITALITY CHECK
Beyond values and skills: Are they CONTRIBUTING TO CULTURE?
Some technically competent people are cultural cancers.
One cultural cancer can destroy a high-performing team.
The rule: No "brilliant jerks." The brilliance doesn't compensate.
```

## THE TRANSITION CONVERSATION

```
"I want to talk to you about your future here.
I care about you and I want to be honest.
I've noticed [specific observations].
The role requires [specific requirements].
The gap between what I'm observing and what's needed is [describe].

I want to give you [timeframe] to [specific improvement].
If we get there: [what success looks like].
If we don't: I want to make sure we both have time to plan appropriately.

Do you have any questions? What support do you need from me?"

THEN: Follow through on both outcomes.
If they improve: Acknowledge it specifically. Keep them.
If they don't: Honor the commitment. Make the transition.
```
