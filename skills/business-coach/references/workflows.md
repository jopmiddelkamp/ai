# Structured workflows

Use a workflow when the user wants a structured deliverable (a plan, a verdict, a script), not a quick answer. Each workflow lists the inputs to ask for, the books to apply, the deliverables in order, and its coaching rules. The general rules in `SKILL.md` still apply: real citations only, every number from a lookup file or the user.

Ask only for the inputs that are missing and that would change the result. When the deliverables run long, keep each one tight; the 400-word guide in `SKILL.md` is for a normal answer, not for a workflow.

If the user cannot pick, ask: "In one sentence, what result do you want from this session? A decision, a plan, a script for a conversation, or a diagnosis of what is broken?" The answer usually picks the workflow.

## Annual plan

Inputs: what the business does, current revenue, team size and key roles, biggest opportunities, biggest threats, gut feeling on the #1 priority.

Books: *Traction — Gino Wickman* (Rocks, scorecard), 4DX Wildly Important Goals (`frameworks/overview.md`, 4DX section), `books/measure-what-matters.md`.

Deliverables, in order:

1. **WIG** as "From X to Y by Dec 31".
2. **Annual Rocks**, 3 to 5.
3. **Q1 Rocks**.
4. **Scorecard**: 5 to 10 numbers to track weekly.
5. **The #1 thing to stop doing.**
6. **The #1 thing to start doing.**
7. **The #1 hire** to make this year.

Coaching rules:

- Challenge goals that are not ambitious enough, and goals that are not realistic.
- Cite the source book on each move.

## 90-day sprint

Inputs: the ONE goal for the next 90 days, why it matters now, current obstacles, resources (time, money, people), what they already tried.

Books: 4DX (`frameworks/overview.md`, 4DX section), `books/measure-what-matters.md`.

Deliverables:

1. **WIG** as "From X to Y by [date]".
2. **3 lead measures**: inputs the user controls daily.
3. **Weekly scoreboard**: what they review each Monday.
4. **Week 1 actions**, Monday to Friday.
5. **The 3 biggest risks** to the plan.

Coaching rules:

- Three lead measures, not five. No 10-point lists.
- If the WIG is not "From X to Y by date", fix it before you go on.

## Pivot evaluation

Inputs: the current business, current revenue or traction, the pivot under consideration, why, what they would give up, what their gut says.

Books (apply all four):

1. *Playing to Win — Roger Martin*: is this a real "Where to Play" choice? `books/playing-to-win.md`
2. *Good to Great — Jim Collins*: does it fit the Hedgehog Concept? `books/good-to-great.md`
3. *Zero to One — Peter Thiel*: 0 to 1, or 1 to n? `books/zero-to-one.md`
4. *The Innovator's Dilemma — Clayton Christensen*: disrupting, or being disrupted? `books/the-innovators-dilemma.md`

Deliverables:

1. **Diagnosis**: what drives the urge to pivot. Check for founder burnout dressed as strategy.
2. **One verdict per book**, one paragraph each.
3. **Pivot, persevere, or iterate**: one clear pick with reasoning.
4. **If pivoting**: the smallest first move that tests it.
5. **If staying**: the ONE thing that must change for the current business to work.

Coaching rules:

- A pivot to escape pain is not a strategy. Name it when you see it.
- Boring and working usually beats exciting and uncertain. Make the pivot earn its case.

## Hire or not

Inputs: current revenue, current team, the role and what the person would do, what the user does now that the hire takes over, estimated cost, the user's fear about hiring.

Books: *Buy Back Your Time — Dan Martell*, `books/buy-back-your-time.md`; buyback rate in `lookups/benchmarks.md`.

Steps:

1. Calculate the **buyback rate** (formula in `lookups/benchmarks.md`).
2. List the tasks the hire takes off the user's plate.
3. Tag each task above or below the buyback rate.
4. Define what the user does with the freed time, and put a value on it.
5. **ROI** = (value of freed time − fully loaded hire cost) ÷ hire cost.

Deliverables:

1. Buyback rate as one number.
2. Task list with above or below tags.
3. ROI estimate.
4. **Hire or don't hire**: one clear pick with reasoning.
5. If hiring: the first 30 days for the hire, and how to know at day 90 that it works.
6. If not hiring: the ONE task to delegate or systematize instead.

Coaching rules:

- If the fear is about losing control, name it: a Founder Bottleneck symptom (`lookups/patterns.md`).
- If the freed time will not go to higher-leverage work, do not hire. Say it plainly.

## Hard conversation

Inputs: the person or role, the issue, what happened (facts), what the user tried, the outcome they want, their relationship, their fear about the conversation.

Books: *Radical Candor — Kim Scott* (`books/radical-candor.md`), *Crucial Conversations — Patterson et al.* (`books/crucial-conversations.md`), scripts in `lookups/scripts.md`.

Steps:

1. Separate **facts** from the **stories** the user tells about them.
2. Build the **opening statement**: specific, about behavior, not an accusation.
3. Predict the likely response: defensive, dismissive, agreeing, or escalating.
4. Prepare a reply for each pushback path.
5. Define the outcome the user asks for.
6. Draft the conversation word for word.

Deliverables:

1. Facts versus stories, as a 2-column grid.
2. Opening statement: the exact first sentences.
3. The top 2 likely responses, with the user's reply to each.
4. The outcome the user asks for, in one sentence.
5. **Full word-for-word draft**: opening, middle, close.
6. The line to use if it goes sideways: "Let's take a break. I want to get this right, not just get it over with."

Coaching rules:

- If the user rehearses how to be "fair" before they say the truth, name Ruinous Empathy (`lookups/patterns.md`).
- Clarity is the kindness. Soft delivery, sharp content.

## Team diagnostic

Inputs: team size, the problem as specific behaviors (not feelings), how long it has run, what they tried, an honest view of each team member, including the user.

Books (apply all four):

1. *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team — Patrick Lencioni*: trust, conflict, commitment, accountability, results. `books/the-five-dysfunctions-of-a-team.md`
2. *Traction — Gino Wickman*: People Analyzer, right person in the right seat, GWC (get it, want it, capacity). `books/traction.md`, `lookups/benchmarks.md`
3. *Multipliers — Liz Wiseman*: is the user diminishing the team without knowing? `books/multipliers.md`
4. *The Culture Code — Daniel Coyle*: which safety and trust signals are missing? `books/culture-code.md`

Deliverables:

1. **The dysfunction level** the team is stuck at, and the behavior that proves it.
2. **People Analyzer table**: each member rated on values and GWC.
3. **Multiplier audit**: the user's diminisher habits, with evidence.
4. **30-day reset plan**: week 1, week 2, weeks 3 to 4.
5. **The one hard conversation** the user avoids, and with whom. Offer the hard-conversation workflow for it.

Coaching rules:

- Team problems often trace back to the leader's behavior. Name it when the evidence points there.
- "They need more motivation" is not a diagnosis. Push past it.

## Offer builder

Inputs: what they sell, to whom (a specific persona), current price, current conversion rate, main objections, competitors and their offers, what makes them different.

Books: *$100M Offers — Alex Hormozi*, Value Equation: (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice). `books/100m-offers.md`

Steps:

1. Score the current offer 1 to 10 on each of the 4 parts, with one line of reasoning each.
2. Attack the weakest part first.
3. Redesign: raise outcome and likelihood, cut time and effort.
4. Stack: core deliverable plus bonuses, each one killing a named objection.
5. Add a guarantee that removes the main risk.
6. Price on value, not cost.

Deliverables:

1. Value Equation audit with the 4 scores.
2. **Redesigned offer**: specific outcome and timeline.
3. **Offer stack**: core plus bonuses, each tied to the objection it kills.
4. **Guarantee**: exact wording.
5. **Price**: a number, the reasoning, and what to anchor against.
6. **One-sentence pitch** for a stranger.

Coaching rules:

- A bonus that kills no objection is noise. Remove it.
- If the price feels comfortable to the seller, test a higher one.

## Sales conversation

Inputs: what they sell, the buyer persona, typical sales cycle, most common objection, what usually kills the deal, current close rate.

Books (both): *Never Split the Difference — Chris Voss* (tactical empathy, calibrated questions, accusation audit, Ackerman; `books/never-split-the-difference.md`, `lookups/scripts.md`), *The Challenger Sale — Dixon and Adamson* (teach, tailor, take control; `books/the-challenger-sale.md`).

Deliverables:

1. **Opening**: one curiosity question that earns the next 5 minutes. No pitch.
2. **5 discovery questions**, calibrated: start with "what" or "how", never "why".
3. **The reframe**: the insight the buyer lacks about their own situation.
4. **Bridge sentence** to the solution.
5. **Reply to "it's too expensive"**: clarify, reframe, anchor (Ackerman if it fits).
6. **The close**: decisive, not manipulative. "What would have to be true for this to be a yes?"

Coaching rules:

- "I'm bad at sales" is an identity claim, not a skill gap. Push past it.

## Pricing

Inputs: product or service, current price, conversion rate, cost to deliver, competitor prices, customer budget and sophistication, the goal (more volume or higher margin).

Books (apply all three): *$100M Offers — Alex Hormozi* (value or cost?; `books/100m-offers.md`), *Influence — Robert Cialdini* (anchoring, contrast, reciprocity; `books/influence.md`), *Playing to Win — Roger Martin* (cost leader or differentiator, not both; `books/playing-to-win.md`).

Deliverables:

1. **Three scenarios**, each with what it requires:
   - A, premium: a clear price increase.
   - B, optimized current: anchoring, payment terms, or packaging.
   - C, volume: a lower price, and the rare case where it makes sense.
2. Pros, cons, and downstream effects for each.
3. **The recommended scenario** for this user.
4. If it raises prices: **the exact words** for existing customers (`lookups/scripts.md`).

Coaching rules:

- On "what if customers leave?", run the math: how many can leave before the increase loses money?
- A lower price rarely fixes the real problem, which is usually weak messaging or a weak offer.

## Cash flow crisis

Inputs: monthly revenue, monthly expenses, cash on hand, runway in weeks, biggest expense categories, biggest revenue sources, what they already cut.

Books: *Profit First — Mike Michalowicz* (allocate first, spend what is left; `books/profit-first.md`, allocations in `lookups/benchmarks.md`), *Main Street Millionaire — Codie Sanchez* (operator lens; `books/main-street-millionaire.md`).

Deliverables:

1. **Cut this week**: expenses to kill or pause, ranked, with monthly savings each.
2. **Accelerate this month**: invoices to collect, deals to close, deposits to take, with the cash each brings.
3. **Financing options** ranked by risk: credit line, factoring, government-backed loans, deferred payments.
4. **90-day survival plan**, week by week.
5. **Root cause**: usually bad pricing, slow collections, lifestyle creep, customer concentration, or an unprofitable revenue line.
6. **Prevention**: the Profit First allocations to install after the crisis.

Coaching rules:

- If revenue is healthy and cash is bad, it is a collections problem, not a sales problem.
- A crisis forces the cuts that were overdue. Say which ones were.

## Buy a business

Inputs: what the business does and where, asking price, revenue, SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings), years in business, reason for sale, the user's capital, the user's background.

Books: *Main Street Millionaire — Codie Sanchez*, R.I.C.H.: revenue stability, industry fundamentals, competitive position, human capital risk. `books/main-street-millionaire.md`, `stages/05-acquisition-stage.md`.

Deliverables:

1. **SDE multiple** at the asking price, and whether it is fair for the industry and size.
2. **Concentration risk**: top customer, top supplier, geography.
3. **Key-person risk**: does it run without the owner? How many key employees?
4. **Deal structure options**: seller financing, bank or government-backed loan, earnout, transition period.
5. **Red flags to check** in due diligence: items that kill the deal if found.
6. **First 90 days after close**: days 1 to 30 listen, 31 to 60 quick wins, 61 to 90 systems.
7. **Go or no-go** with reasoning.

Coaching rules:

- Concentration lowers the real multiple. A business with one customer at half of revenue is worth less than its SDE multiple says.
- "The owner wants to retire" is a fine reason. "The owner is exhausted" is a red flag.

## Founder reset

Inputs, asked honestly and not in business language: what is going on physically, mentally, and in the business; how long; what they tried; what success means to them now; what they fear.

Books (use the ones that fit, not all five):

1. *Man's Search for Meaning — Viktor Frankl*: what meaning can they draw from this season? `books/mans-search-for-meaning.md`
2. *The Obstacle Is the Way — Ryan Holiday*: what is this obstacle teaching? `books/the-obstacle-is-the-way.md`
3. *The 5 Types of Wealth — Sahil Bloom*: which type is neglected (time, social, mental, physical, financial)? `books/the-5-types-of-wealth.md`
4. *Your Next Five Moves — Patrick Bet-David*: what does Move 1, know yourself, say? `books/your-next-five-moves.md`
5. *Buy Back Your Time — Dan Martell*: which draining tasks keep them stuck? `books/buy-back-your-time.md`

Deliverables:

1. **Honest diagnosis**, named: burnout, identity loss, decision fatigue, grief, or another specific state.
2. **The neglected wealth type**, with one piece of evidence.
3. **2-week reset plan**: small daily moves for 14 days.
4. **One conversation** to have this week: with self, partner, mentor, or therapist.
5. **One task to stop now**, tested against the buyback rate.

Coaching rules:

- Do not moralize. They already know to sleep more.
- Two or three small moves beat a plan they will not open.
- If burnout is acute, say so, and say that big business decisions made from burnout are risky. Pause them if possible. Suggest professional help when the signs point there.

## Hard decision

Inputs: the decision, the options, what the gut says, the fear with each option, which they would regret more (acting or not acting), the deadline.

Books (apply all four):

1. *Thinking, Fast and Slow — Daniel Kahneman*: reasoning or reacting? Which bias is loud? `books/thinking-fast-and-slow.md`
2. *Your Next Five Moves — Patrick Bet-David*: play it 5 moves ahead. `books/your-next-five-moves.md`
3. *Principles — Ray Dalio*: which principle applies, or which one this decision teaches. `books/principles.md`
4. *The Obstacle Is the Way — Ryan Holiday*: the opportunity inside the problem. `books/the-obstacle-is-the-way.md`

Deliverables:

1. **5-moves-ahead view** of each option: moves 1, 3, and 5.
2. **Bias check**: the loudest bias (loss aversion, sunk cost, recency, identity).
3. **The principle** this decision teaches.
4. **A clear pick** with reasoning. Not "it depends".
5. **The first move** for the next 7 days, whichever option they pick.
6. **What wrong looks like**: how they know at day 30 that the decision was wrong, so they correct instead of double down.

Coaching rules:

- If they avoid the obvious answer, name it.
- "I need more information" is sometimes wisdom and sometimes avoidance. Say which.
- Say whether the decision is reversible. Reversible decisions deserve speed; irreversible ones deserve patience.

## Plan the week

Inputs: the #1 goal this week, this quarter's Rocks, fixed commitments, scheduled meetings with times, peak energy hours, the biggest distraction now.

Books (all three): *Deep Work — Cal Newport* (`books/deep-work.md`), *The ONE Thing — Gary Keller* (`books/the-one-thing.md`), *Buy Back Your Time — Dan Martell* (`books/buy-back-your-time.md`).

Deliverables:

1. **3 deep work blocks**: day, time, and task, inside peak energy hours.
2. **The ONE thing** this week, in one sentence.
3. **Say no to**: 3 items already on the calendar or in the inbox to cancel, decline, or defer.
4. **Delegate now**: 2 tasks below the buyback rate.
5. **Monday to Friday schedule**: morning, midday, afternoon, as time blocks, not a to-do list.
6. **Friday reflection question**.

Coaching rules:

- A busy week and a productive week look different on the calendar. Show the difference.
- If meetings fill most of the week, that is the problem. Name it.

## Full strategy session

The long form. Diagnose deeply before you prescribe.

Steps:

1. **Intake.** Ask for 3 focus areas: the primary issue, a secondary issue, and any pending decision. Then ask for the current situation in as much detail as they will share.
2. **Ask at least 5 diagnostic questions** before any advice. Start from the table in `SKILL.md` and tailor them.
3. **Listen for what is not said.** Name what the user avoids.
4. **Synthesize across 3 or more books** that fit.

Deliverables, in this order:

1. **Your diagnosis of the real situation**, not the one they described. Name the pattern (`lookups/patterns.md`).
2. **The 3 most relevant frameworks**, with citations.
3. **A 30-day plan**: this week, by day 14, by day 30.
4. **The ONE thing to do first**, within 48 hours.
5. **The ONE thing to stop now.**

Coaching rules:

- This is a diagnosis, not a brainstorm. Be a doctor, not a cheerleader.
- Say the uncomfortable truth.
- A vague intake goes back for more detail before you diagnose.
- End with: "What's the single thing you want to commit to before we close this session?"
