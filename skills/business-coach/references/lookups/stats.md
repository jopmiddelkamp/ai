# Stats Arsenal

Specific, citable numbers paired with their sources. Use them to **support** a hard truth — never as a substitute for diagnosis, and never as a measurement of the user's own situation.

**Rule of use:** A statistic illustrates a *pattern in the broader population*. The user is one data point. Cite a stat to give a hard truth weight, then return to their specific situation.

---

## Validation & Failure

- **42% of failed startups cite "no market need"** as the primary failure reason. *CB Insights, post-mortem analysis of 100+ failed startups.* Use for: build-before-validate conversations.

## Greatness & Leadership

- **Collins studied 1,435 companies over 40 years** (Fortune 500 lists 1965-1995). **Only 11 made the leap from good to great.** That's 0.77%. *Good to Great — Collins.* Use for: setting realistic expectations about what "great" actually requires.

- **Multipliers get 95% of people's intelligence; Diminishers get 48%.** Same humans, different leaders, roughly 2x the cognitive output. *Multipliers — Wiseman.* Use for: leadership style conversations.

## Decision Speed

- **Most leaders know within 6 months when something isn't working. Most wait 3 years to act.** *Good to Great — Collins.* Use for: pushing decisions on underperforming hires, channels, products.

## Compounding & Effort

- **1% daily improvement compounds to ~37x in a year** (1.01³⁶⁵ ≈ 37.78). **1% daily decline compounds to near zero** (0.99³⁶⁵ ≈ 0.03). *Atomic Habits — Clear.* Use for: pushing back on "I don't have time for this" objections.

- **The 40% Rule:** When your mind says "I'm done," you are at roughly 40% of capacity. *Can't Hurt Me — Goggins.* Use for: burnout vs. quit conversations; distinguishing "I can't" from "I won't."

- **Effort counts twice:** Talent × Effort = Skill. Skill × Effort = Achievement. *Grit — Duckworth.* Use for: hiring decisions, performance reviews, "do I have what it takes" questions.

## How to use a stat in a response

**Bad:** "42% of startups fail because of no market need, so you should validate."

**Good:** "You're building before validating. *CB Insights* puts 'no market need' as the #1 reason failed startups cite — 42% of them. You're about to spend 3-6 months and $5-10K on an idea validated by one family member. The Mom Test exists specifically because this pattern kills more businesses than bad products do. *The Mom Test — Fitzpatrick*"

The difference: the second version names the pattern, cites the stat as evidence, quantifies their specific cost, and points to the book that solves it.

**Do not invent statistics.** If you don't have a specific cited number, don't fabricate one — describe the dynamic qualitatively instead.
