# Conversation Scripts

Word-for-word language for the conversations that founders actually have to have. When the situation involves talking to another person — an employee, a customer, a partner — generic advice ("have an honest conversation") fails. Give the script.

Adapt the wording to the user's voice and relationship, but keep the structure.

---

## Hard Feedback (Radical Candor)

> *"I want to talk about your future here, because I care about your growth — not just what you produce for us. You told me [specific issue]. I take that seriously. I don't want you to stay somewhere that stunts you. But I think there's a version of this that would genuinely stretch you. Can I share what I'm thinking?"*

Then **stop talking and listen.** Aim for a roughly 2:1 listen-to-talk ratio for the rest of the conversation.

**Why it works:** It leads with Care Personally (their growth, not your needs), names the specific issue without hedging, and creates space for them to respond instead of presenting a conclusion. *Radical Candor — Kim Scott.*

---

## Customer Discovery (Mom Test)

**Do not ask:** "Would you buy this?" or "Do you like this idea?" These produce polite lies.

**Ask instead, in this order:**

> *"What's the hardest part about [problem area] right now?"*
> *"Tell me about the last time that frustrated you."*
> *"What did you do about it?"*
> *"How much have you spent trying to solve it?"*

**Why it works:** Questions are about their past behavior, not their predicted future behavior. Past behavior is data. Predicted behavior is wishful thinking. *The Mom Test — Fitzpatrick.*

---

## Price Increase

> *"As of [date], our pricing will be [new price]. This allows us to [specific investment that benefits them — better service, better team, faster delivery, etc.]. I wanted you to hear this directly from me."*

**Do not apologize. Do not negotiate against yourself before they push back.** State it as a decision that has already been made.

**If they push back:** Listen fully. Then either hold (most common) or, if the relationship genuinely warrants it, offer a time-bound transition. Never reverse the decision under emotional pressure in the same conversation.

**Why it works:** Specificity and finality signal calculation, not arbitrariness. *Never Split the Difference — Voss.*

**Caveat:** This script assumes a healthy book of business. If a single customer is >20% of revenue, the dynamics change — sequence the announcement carefully, and have a fallback plan.

---

## Reframing a Sale (Challenger)

> *"Most [customer type] believe [common assumption in their industry]. What we've found working with 200+ companies is actually [counterintuitive insight]. That's because [mechanism — why the common assumption breaks down]. The implication for you is [specific consequence in their situation]."*

**Why it works:** Leads with teaching, not pitching. The customer learns something true before they hear what you sell. *The Challenger Sale.*

---

## Negotiation (Ackerman)

Anchor at 65% of your target, then move to 85%, then 95%, then 100%.

- Use **non-round numbers** ($127,500 not $130,000). Specificity signals you've done the math.
- **Never accept the first counter.**
- Each move should look smaller than the last, signaling you are approaching your true limit.

**Why it works:** The pattern of declining concessions feels natural and resembles a real bottom. *Never Split the Difference — Voss.*

---

## Accusation Audit

Before delivering hard news, name every negative thing the other person is about to think — out loud, first.

> *"You're probably thinking this is just another price increase. You're probably thinking we don't value the relationship. You're probably thinking we're going to do this again in six months..."*

Then deliver the news.

**Why it works:** Saying their objections out loud first defangs them — they cannot accuse you of something you've already named. *Never Split the Difference — Voss.*

---

## How to choose a script

| If the situation involves... | Use |
|---|---|
| Telling someone hard truth about their performance | Radical Candor |
| Talking to potential customers about a problem | Mom Test |
| Telling existing customers about a price change | Price Increase + Accusation Audit |
| Selling something complex to a skeptical buyer | Challenger reframe |
| Buying, selling, or negotiating any deal | Ackerman + Tactical Empathy |
| Delivering bad news of any kind | Accusation Audit first |
