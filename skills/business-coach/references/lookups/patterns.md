# Pattern Library

Named anti-patterns and traps. The user cannot fix what they cannot name — naming the pattern is the first move. Always pair the pattern with its book citation.

**Rule:** Use a named pattern when it genuinely fits the user's situation. If none of the patterns below describe what's actually happening, describe the dynamic plainly — do not force-fit.

---

## Founder & Role

**Technician's Trap** — Doing $15/hr work while $500/hr strategy goes undone. The founder is stuck in the doer role and the business cannot grow past their personal output. *E-Myth Revisited — Gerber*

**Founder as Bottleneck** — Decisions wait until the founder is available. The team asks permission instead of owning outcomes. Quality drops when the founder isn't watching. *Buy Back Your Time — Martell*

**Identity Attachment** — Cannot delegate because identity is tied to being the doer. "Only I can do this right" is the conscious version; the unconscious version is "if someone else can do this, who am I?" *E-Myth + Buy Back Your Time*

---

## Validation & Build

**Build-Before-Validate** — Spending months building before talking to customers. 42% of failed startups cite "no market need" as the reason. *Lean Startup — Ries*

**The Mom Test Trap** — Asking "Would you buy this?" and getting polite lies in return. Friends, family, and casual contacts will validate almost anything. *The Mom Test — Fitzpatrick*

---

## Team & Culture

**Artificial Harmony** — Teams being polite instead of honest. Real disagreement gets pushed into hallway conversations. Level 2 of the Five Dysfunctions pyramid. *Lencioni*

**Ruinous Empathy** — Caring too much to tell hard truths, so people never get the feedback they need to improve. The Care Personally axis without the Challenge Directly axis. *Radical Candor — Kim Scott*

**Competence Ceiling** — A top performer has maxed out what the role can teach them and is starting to disengage or look elsewhere. Promote, expand the role, or lose them. *Multipliers — Wiseman*

---

## Revenue & Risk

**Revenue Concentration Risk** — Any single customer above 20% of revenue is an existential vulnerability. Losing them takes the business with them.

**Key Person Dependency** — More than 40% of revenue lives in one person's head (often the founder). The company is functionally unsellable in that state. *Main Street Millionaire — Sanchez*

---

## Strategy & Focus

**Doom Loop** — Lurching between new initiatives with no compounding momentum. The opposite of the flywheel. *Good to Great — Collins*

**The Channel Trap** — Trying to execute every marketing channel simultaneously instead of mastering one. Result: mediocre presence everywhere, dominance nowhere.

**The 60-70% Trap** — Accepting "good enough" opportunities that crowd out exceptional ones. McKeown's rule: "If it isn't a clear yes, it's a clear no." *Essentialism — McKeown*

---

## How to use a pattern in a response

1. **Name it explicitly** — "You're in the Technician's Trap."
2. **Cite the source** — italics: *E-Myth Revisited — Gerber*.
3. **Connect to their situation in one sentence** — "You said you're working 70-hour weeks and revenue plateaued at $400K; that's the textbook signature."
4. **Pivot to the move** — name the pattern, then give the specific next step.

The name is the insight. The citation is the proof. The move is the value.
