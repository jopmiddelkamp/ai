# Key Benchmarks

Specific thresholds that separate "expert advice" from "vague guidance." When a benchmark applies to the user's situation, cite the number — but always with the caveat that benchmarks vary by industry, model, and stage.

---

## Unit Economics

**LTV:CAC ratio — should be at least 3:1.** Below that, you are buying customers at a loss in the long run. Above 5:1 may mean you are underspending on growth.

**Gross margin by industry (rough):**
- Software / SaaS: 70%+
- Professional services: 50–70%
- Physical products: 30–50%
- Restaurants: 60–70% (food cost basis)

If margins are below these ranges, fix margins before chasing growth — you are scaling losses.

---

## Concentration & Risk

**Revenue concentration:** No single customer should exceed **20% of revenue**. Above that line, the business has existential exposure to a single relationship.

**Key person dependency:** If more than **40% of revenue or operations** lives in one person's head, the business is functionally unsellable until that knowledge is extracted into systems. *Main Street Millionaire — Sanchez.*

---

## Founder Time

**Buyback Rate:** Annual income goal ÷ 2,000 hours = your hourly rate. **Delegate everything below it.** A founder targeting $400K/year has a buyback rate of $200/hr — bookkeeping, scheduling, and inbox triage should not be on their calendar. *Buy Back Your Time — Martell.*

**The 2-week test:** Can the business run for 2 weeks without you? If no, you ARE the bottleneck — not "a" bottleneck.

---

## Offer Quality

**Offer score (Value Equation rubric):** Rate the offer on each of four levers, 1–10:
1. Dream Outcome
2. Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
3. Time Delay
4. Effort & Sacrifice

Below 30/40 = the offer needs serious work before scaling spend on it. 35–40 = "grand slam" territory. *$100M Offers — Hormozi.*

**Value Equation formula:**

> VALUE = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)

To improve value, pull any of the four levers — but the denominator (less time, less effort) is usually higher leverage than the numerator.

---

## Profit & Cash

**Profit First allocations** (starting target; adjust by industry):
- Profit: 5–20% (taken FIRST, into a separate account)
- Owner Pay: ~50%
- Tax: ~15%
- Operating Expenses: remainder

The discipline is the order of operations: profit comes out before expenses, not after. *Profit First — Michalowicz.*

**Hormozi Core Equation:**

> Profit = (Leads × Conversion × Price × LTV) − Costs

When growth stalls, multiply through the equation to find which lever is actually broken. Most founders default to "we need more leads" when the bottleneck is conversion or price.

## Strategy

**Flywheel question:** "If I had to bet the entire business on ONE metric — one number that, if it grows, forces everything else to grow — what is it?" If you cannot name it in a sentence, you do not yet have a flywheel. *Good to Great — Collins.*

**Hedgehog intersection:** Passion × Best-in-World × Economic Engine. All three must overlap. Two out of three is a hobby or a job. *Good to Great — Collins.*

---

## People

**Keeper Test (Hastings):** *"If they told me they were leaving for a competitor, how hard would I fight to keep them?"*

- Strong fight → keep, invest, expand the role.
- Mixed feelings → put on a clear improvement plan with a deadline.
- Quiet relief → start transitioning them out.

*No Rules Rules — Hastings.*

**GWC test (Wickman):** For every person in every seat, ask:
- **G**ets it — do they understand the role?
- **W**ants it — do they actually want this role, not just the title?
- **C**apacity — do they have the time, talent, and energy to do it?

A "no" on any of the three means a seat problem, not a person problem. *Traction — Wickman.*

---

## How to use a benchmark in a response

1. State the benchmark with its source.
2. Place the user's number against it: above, at, or below.
3. Name the implication concretely — not "you should improve" but "you have a [specific risk] until you get to [specific number]."
4. Give the move that closes the gap.
